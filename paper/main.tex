\begin{abstract}
Automatic road extraction from high-resolution satellite and aerial imagery
supports map updating, logistics planning, and post-disaster response. The task
remains difficult because road surfaces cover only a few percent of the pixels,
form thin and elongated structures that are frequently occluded by trees,
shadows, and vehicles, and are useful only when their connectivity is preserved.
Encoder-decoder networks that downsample deeply discard the evidence for narrow
roads before the decoder can recover it, whereas networks that hold high
resolution throughout are costly at inference time. This paper presents
DBR-Net (Dual-Branch Road Network), a segmentation network built around a detail
stream that never leaves stride~8 and a semantic stream that descends to
stride~32 to gather context. The two streams communicate through a single
bilateral exchange regulated by a zero-initialized spatial gate, so that the
network starts as an exact copy of an ungated residual exchange and acquires
selectivity gradually.
Broad context is aggregated by a progressive pyramid pooling module built on
group normalization and re-enters the detail stream through a controlled fusion
step. Connectivity is supervised by an auxiliary centerline head trained with an
asymmetric Tversky objective whose targets come from a pure-tensor morphological
thinning applied before any downsampling. Every multi-branch block fuses exactly
into a single convolution for deployment. On the Massachusetts Roads and
DeepGlobe benchmarks, evaluated at native resolution with Hann-weighted
sliding-window inference, the proposed network reaches a road IoU of
[Đien ket qua sau] and [Đien ket qua sau], exceeding the strongest baseline
by [DELTA\_MASS] and [DELTA\_DG] percentage points at
90.46~GMACs per 1024$\times$1024 tile after re-parameterization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Road networks are a primary layer of nearly every geospatial information system,
and keeping that layer current is a recurring cost. New construction, closures,
and damage after floods or earthquakes change the network faster than manual
digitization can follow. High-resolution optical satellite and aerial imagery is
now acquired at sub-meter to meter ground sampling distance over most inhabited
areas, which makes automated extraction of road surfaces from such imagery a
practical route to map maintenance \rev{\cite{abdollahi2020roadreview, chen2022roadsurvey, luweng2025roadreview}}. The applications
that consume the result, including routing, logistics, and the allocation of
emergency access after a disaster, depend less on the exact area of the predicted
road surface than on whether the extracted network remains connected.

Road extraction is usually posed as binary semantic segmentation, yet several
properties separate it from the object-centric problems that drive most
architectural progress. Roads occupy roughly two to five percent of the pixels in
a typical scene\rev{~\cite{demir2018deepglobe}}, so an unweighted pixel objective converges toward a near-empty
prediction and pixel accuracy carries almost no information. At one meter per
pixel a two-lane road is between eight and twenty pixels wide; a feature map at
stride 32 represents it with less than one pixel, and no decoder can reliably
reconstruct what the encoder has already discarded. The structures are also
anisotropic and long-range: rivers, field boundaries, fences, and elongated
rooftops are locally indistinguishable from roads, and only regional context
resolves the ambiguity. Occlusion compounds the difficulty\rev{~\cite{mnih2013aerial, cheng2017cascaded, zhou2020btroadnet}}, because a five-pixel
gap caused by a tree canopy leaves the intersection-over-union nearly unchanged
while destroying an entire route. Evaluation protocols that report only pixel
overlap therefore reward the wrong behavior.

Two lines of work address these properties. The first adapts general-purpose
encoder-decoder segmentation networks to aerial imagery, from U-Net
\cite{ronneberger2015unet} and Deep Residual U-Net \cite{zhang2018resunet} to
D-LinkNet \cite{zhou2018dlinknet}, and then improves feature aggregation through
\rev{non-local context \cite{wang2022nllinknet},} multi-resolution exchange
\cite{wang2021hrnet}\rev{,} or attention decoders \rev{\cite{xie2021segformer, wang2022unetformer}}. The
second supervises connectivity directly, either through auxiliary geometric
targets such as road \rev{centerlines \cite{cheng2017cascaded, lu2019msmtre},
local direction \cite{ding2021diresnet},} and
connectivity attention \cite{mei2021coanet}, or through topology-aware objectives
such as \rev{connectivity-oriented losses \cite{oner2022connectivity} and} clDice
\cite{shit2021cldice}\rev{, complemented by imbalance-aware region losses such as}
the asymmetric Tversky loss \cite{salehi2017tversky}.
Section~\ref{sec:related-work} reviews both lines in detail.

Three issues remain unresolved across them. Preserving a high-resolution stream
is effective, yet the usual mechanism, symmetric exchange among all resolution
pairs \cite{wang2021hrnet, yu2021bisenetv2, pan2023ddrnet}, spends part of its
budget on exchanges a thin-structure task cannot use: a stride-32 map cannot
represent an eight-pixel road, so requiring it to participate in a
detail-preserving exchange contributes little. Connectivity supervision is
likewise effective, but published formulations either depend on external
morphological libraries, which complicates reproducibility and distributed
training, or skeletonize after the label has been downsampled, which merges
narrow branches and intersections before they can be supervised. The third issue
is discussed less often: multi-branch blocks and parallel context modules that
improve accuracy also slow inference on the large scenes operational mapping
requires, and structural re-parameterization \rev{\cite{ding2019acnet, ding2021repvgg}}, which removes
that cost exactly, has to our knowledge not been combined with a road-specific
detail-preserving design. An architecture is needed in which the detail stream is
preserved by construction, the semantic stream contributes only where it helps,
connectivity is supervised without external dependencies, and the multi-branch
machinery collapses into a single-path network for deployment.

DBR-Net is designed around those requirements. A detail stream is
anchored at stride~8 and never descends further, so evidence for narrow roads is
never destroyed. A semantic stream anchored at stride~16 reaches stride~32 only
to collect context, and exactly one bilateral exchange, between stride~8 and
stride~16, connects the two. Each direction of that exchange is a residual term
modulated by a single-channel spatial gate whose final convolution is
zero-initialized, so the gate evaluates to one at initialization and the network
begins as an exact copy of the ungated variant, leaving the pretrained feature
distribution undisturbed. Context is aggregated by a progressive pyramid module
that adds pooling scales from fine to coarse and uses group normalization
\rev{\cite{wu2020groupnorm}}, which keeps the global pooling branch valid at the small
per-device batch sizes imposed by 1024-pixel crops. The context returns to the
detail stream through a fusion step that normalizes the two streams separately
and admits semantics through a learnable per-channel scale. Connectivity is
supervised by an auxiliary centerline head trained with a Tversky loss
\cite{salehi2017tversky} whose targets are generated by morphological thinning
implemented entirely with max-pooling and applied before any downsampling. All
multi-branch blocks are re-parameterizable \cite{ding2021repvgg} and fuse into
single convolutions after training.

The experimental program evaluates the model on the Massachusetts Roads dataset
\cite{mnih2013aerial} and the DeepGlobe road extraction dataset
\cite{demir2018deepglobe}, which differ in ground sampling distance, scene
composition, and image size. Every evaluation runs at native resolution with
overlapping tiles blended by a Hann window in the logit domain, so reported
scores are not inflated by resizing. The primary metric is the
intersection-over-union of the road class alone; a relaxed F1 score with a
three-pixel tolerance is reported alongside it to separate route quality from
boundary precision. Ablations isolate the spatial gate, the centerline objective,
the occlusion augmentation, and the progressive context module, and a separate
study quantifies the accuracy and compute effect of re-parameterization.

The contributions of this work are the following.

\begin{enumerate}
\item An asymmetric dual-resolution architecture for road extraction in which a
      stride-8 detail stream is preserved by construction and a single gated
      bilateral exchange, rather than an all-pairs exchange, couples it to a
      semantic stream.
\item A zero-initialized single-channel spatial gate that makes the gated
      exchange an exact extension of an ungated residual exchange at
      initialization, so selectivity is learned without perturbing pretrained
      features.
\item A centerline supervision scheme whose targets are produced by
      max-pooling-based morphological thinning applied before downsampling and
      optimized with an asymmetric Tversky loss that penalizes broken roads more
      heavily than spurious ones.
\item A road-guided occlusion augmentation that draws shadows, vegetation, and
      vehicles onto labeled road pixels while leaving the target mask unchanged,
      which converts occlusion into an explicit reconstruction objective.
\item An evaluation and deployment protocol combining native-resolution
      Hann-blended inference, manifest-recorded splits, validation-only threshold
      calibration, and structural re-parameterization verified to float32
      round-off.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Encoder-decoder networks for road extraction}
\label{subsec:rw-encdec}

\rev{Mnih~\cite{mnih2013aerial} trained neural networks on large aerial image
datasets to label road pixels from a wide image context, because occlusions and
shadows cast by trees and tall buildings often make local cues insufficient, and
released the Massachusetts Roads dataset, which established} patch-based learning of road
masks from aerial imagery as a benchmark task. \rev{Fully convolutional
networks~\cite{shelhamer2017fcn}, trained end to end from pixels to pixels, then moved
segmentation from patch-wise classification to dense prediction over inputs of
arbitrary size.} U-Net
\cite{ronneberger2015unet} introduced the skip-connected encoder-decoder that
most later road models inherit: the encoder builds semantic capacity by
downsampling, and lateral skips return spatial detail to the decoder. Deep
Residual U-Net \cite{zhang2018resunet} \rev{rebuilt this architecture from
residual units} and reported improved road delineation on Massachusetts with fewer
parameters than the original.

The DeepGlobe challenge \cite{demir2018deepglobe} shifted attention to
0.5\,m/pixel satellite imagery and larger scene variety. Its winning entry,
D-LinkNet \cite{zhou2018dlinknet}, \rev{built on the LinkNet architecture with a
pretrained encoder,} inserted a cascade of
dilated convolutions at the encoder bottleneck, enlarging the receptive field
without an additional downsampling stage, and it remains the reference baseline
on that benchmark. \rev{NL-LinkNet \cite{wang2022nllinknet} added non-local
blocks to a LinkNet-based network, so that every location can draw on global
context, and outperformed D-LinkNet with 43\% fewer parameters.} The
shared limitation of this family is structural rather than incidental. Skip
connections return detail that survived the encoder, but a road eight to twenty
pixels wide is represented by a fraction of a pixel at stride 32, so what the
decoder receives through the deepest skips is context, not geometry. Dilation
mitigates the problem by avoiding one downsampling step; it does not remove the
dependence on a deeply downsampled trunk.

\subsection{Multi-resolution and dual-branch architectures}
\label{subsec:rw-multires}

A second design maintains high resolution throughout the network instead of
recovering it. HRNet \cite{wang2021hrnet} runs parallel branches at several
resolutions and repeatedly exchanges information among all of them, which keeps a
high-resolution representation available at every depth. The approach is
effective for position-sensitive tasks, but the exchange is dense: every pair of
resolutions is fused at every stage, and the cost grows with the number of
branches.

Real-time segmentation networks reduce that cost to two branches. BiSeNet~V2
\cite{yu2021bisenetv2} pairs a shallow detail branch with a deeper semantic
branch and merges them once through a guided aggregation layer. DDRNet
\cite{pan2023ddrnet} keeps two resolutions throughout and exchanges between them
repeatedly, and introduces the deep aggregation pyramid pooling module to enlarge
the receptive field at the low-resolution branch. \rev{Both were
developed for real-time scene parsing and benchmarked chiefly on street-level
scenes}, where objects are large and the detail branch mainly
sharpens boundaries. Overhead road imagery inverts that balance: the structures
of interest are themselves near the resolution limit, so which resolutions
exchange, and under what control, matters more than how many exchanges occur.
\rev{DDRNet sums projected features of the two branches without any gating, and
the guided aggregation layer of BiSeNet~V2 multiplies each branch by
sigmoid-activated responses of the other, a dense gate over every channel and
location rather than a single spatial map; neither} is initialized so that
adding the exchange leaves a pretrained encoder undisturbed. \rev{Within road
extraction, DBRANet \cite{chen2022dbranet} places a dual-branch module, a
residual block beside a refined asymmetric block, in the encoder of a U-shaped
network and decodes with a regional attention module; its two branches
strengthen the feature extraction of the encoder, and the network keeps the
encoder-decoder layout discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:rw-encdec}.}

Context aggregation modules developed for scene parsing are used across both
families. Pyramid pooling \cite{zhao2017pspnet} and atrous spatial pyramid
pooling \rev{\cite{chen2018deeplab}} both compute several receptive-field scales
in parallel and combine them once at the end. The deep aggregation variant
\cite{pan2023ddrnet} adds hierarchical residual connections among the pooling
branches. These modules are normally built on batch normalization, which becomes
unreliable at the small per-device batch sizes \rev{\cite{wu2020groupnorm}} that 1024-pixel remote sensing
crops impose, particularly for a branch that pools to a single spatial location.

\subsection{Connectivity and topology-aware supervision}
\label{subsec:rw-topology}

Because pixel overlap is insensitive to short breaks, a separate line of work
supervises connectivity explicitly. \rev{Several road networks predict the
centerline as a second task: CasNet \cite{cheng2017cascaded} cascades a
centerline extraction network onto a road detection network and thins its output
into a single-pixel-wide centerline network, RCNN-UNet \cite{yang2019rcnnunet}
and MSMT-RE \cite{lu2019msmtre} train road detection and centerline extraction
jointly in U-Net-based multi-task models, and RoadNet \cite{liu2019roadnet}
predicts road surfaces, edges, and centerlines simultaneously. DiResNet
\cite{ding2021diresnet} supervises local road directions at the pixel level and
adds a structural supervision and a refinement network to improve road
topology, and BT-RoadNet \cite{zhou2020btroadnet} refines a coarse road map with
a second encoder-decoder and uses a spatial context module to counter
discontinuities.} CoANet
\cite{mei2021coanet} matches the network to road geometry directly, using strip
convolutions along four directions together with a connectivity attention module
that predicts whether neighboring locations belong to the same road. \rev{A
further line abandons the raster mask and builds the road graph directly:
RNGDet \cite{xu2022rngdet} generates the road network graph vertex by vertex from
an aerial image with a transformer, motivated by the limited topological
correctness of segmentation-based approaches. The present work stays
within the raster formulation, whose consequences are discussed in
Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}.}

Loss functions attack the same problem without changing the architecture. Region
losses of the Dice family \cite{milletari2016vnet} and reweighted classification
losses \rev{\cite{lin2020focal}} counteract the imbalance itself, and the Tversky loss
\cite{salehi2017tversky} generalizes Dice with separate weights for false
positives and false negatives, so an application can declare which error is worse.
\rev{Oner et al.~\cite{oner2022connectivity} express road connectivity through
the background regions that a gap in the prediction wrongly merges, and penalize
such connections so that the gaps close.}
clDice \cite{shit2021cldice} measures overlap between the skeletons of prediction
and label, which makes a break expensive even when it costs few pixels, and
provides a differentiable soft skeletonization built from morphological
operations.

Two practical constraints in this literature motivate the present design. First,
skeleton-based supervision is often implemented with external morphological
libraries operating on CPU arrays, which complicates distributed training and
makes the target dependent on a library version. Second, when the auxiliary head
sits at a reduced stride, the natural implementation downsamples the label and
then thins it; that order merges narrow branches and intersections, which are
precisely the structures the term is meant to protect.

\subsection{Structural re-parameterization}
\label{subsec:rw-reparam}

Multi-branch blocks improve optimization but cost inference time. \rev{ACNet
\cite{ding2019acnet} strengthened square kernels with parallel one-dimensional
asymmetric convolutions during training and converted the trained network back
into its original architecture, so the added branches cost nothing at
inference.} RepVGG
\cite{ding2021repvgg} showed that a training-time block of parallel $3\times3$,
$1\times1$, and identity paths, each followed by batch normalization, can be
folded algebraically into a single $3\times3$ convolution, so the deployed model
is a plain sequential stack with no accuracy loss. The transformation is exact:
batch normalization folds into the preceding convolution, and kernels padded to a
common size sum linearly.

Re-parameterization has been applied mainly to classification backbones
\rev{\cite{ding2019acnet, ding2021repvgg}} and
general-purpose detectors. Its combination with a detail-preserving segmentation
design for remote sensing, where inference runs over thousands of overlapping
tiles per scene and branch-level memory traffic is a real cost, has to our
knowledge not been reported. The blocks used here follow the same algebra\rev{;
like ACNet, they} add
anisotropic $1\times5$ and $5\times1$ depthwise branches, chosen for the
elongated geometry of road segments \rev{in the spirit of the strip convolutions
of CoANet \cite{mei2021coanet}}, that fuse into a single $5\times5$ depthwise
kernel.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Problem formulation and notation}
\label{subsec:formulation}

Let $\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times H \times W}$ denote an optical RGB image
of height $H$ and width $W$, and let
$\mathbf{Y} \in \{0,1\}^{H \times W}$ denote the corresponding label, where
$Y_{ij} = 1$ marks a road-surface pixel. The task is to learn a mapping
$f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ that produces two-channel logits at the input
resolution,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:task}
\mathbf{Z} = f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{I}) \in
\mathbb{R}^{2 \times H \times W},
\end{equation}
%
where $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ collects all trainable parameters. The road
probability map $\mathbf{P} \in [0,1]^{H \times W}$ and the predicted mask
$\hat{\mathbf{Y}}$ follow from the channel-wise softmax and a decision threshold
$\tau$,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:threshold}
P_{ij} = \frac{\exp(Z_{1ij})}{\exp(Z_{0ij}) + \exp(Z_{1ij})},
\qquad
\hat{Y}_{ij} = \mathbb{1}\!\left[P_{ij} \ge \tau\right],
\end{equation}
%
with $\mathbb{1}[\cdot]$ the indicator function. The default threshold is
$\tau = 0.5$; Section~\ref{subsec:metrics} describes how a calibrated threshold
is estimated on validation data only.

Feature tensors are written $\mathbf{F}^{(s)}
\in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H/s \times W/s}$, where the superscript is the output
stride $s$ relative to the input. Scalars are italic lowercase, vectors bold
lowercase, and tensors bold uppercase. Throughout the paper, $\mathbf{D}$ denotes
the detail stream and $\mathbf{S}$ the semantic stream.

\label{subsec:overview}

DBR-Net is assembled from five modules on top of a shared encoder, as
shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}. The encoder
(Section~\ref{subsec:encoder}) extracts five feature levels from the input tile.
The dual-resolution context module (Section~\ref{subsec:dualres}) splits those
features into a detail stream held at stride~8 and a semantic stream anchored at
stride~16, and couples them through one bilateral exchange whose two directions
are modulated by residual spatial gates (Section~\ref{subsec:gate}). Broad
context is collected at stride~32 by a progressive pyramid pooling module
(Section~\ref{subsec:dappm}) and returned to the semantic stream. The two streams
merge in a controlled fusion module (Section~\ref{subsec:fusion}), and a
reconstruction decoder (Section~\ref{subsec:decoder}) restores the full input
resolution and emits the logits of Eq.~\eqref{eq:task}. Every convolutional
refinement inside these modules is a re-parameterizable block
(Section~\ref{subsec:reparam}) that collapses to a single convolution after
training.

The organizing principle is an asymmetry between the two streams. The detail
stream is responsible for spatial fidelity and is never downsampled, so evidence
for a road eight to twenty pixels wide is preserved end to end. The semantic
stream is responsible for regional context and is never asked to preserve thin
structures, which is why it is free to descend to stride~32. Consequently there
is exactly one genuine bilateral exchange in the network, between stride~8 and
stride~16, rather than an exchange between every pair of resolutions as in
HRNet~\cite{wang2021hrnet}. Information passes from stride~32 to the detail
stream only indirectly, through the semantic stream, so the coarsest map is never
required to carry road geometry.

\subsection{Re-parameterizable building blocks}
\label{subsec:reparam}

The refinement stages of every module are built from two block types that are
multi-branch during training and single-branch at inference, following the
re-parameterization principle of RepVGG~\cite{ding2021repvgg}. Introducing them
first makes the module descriptions that follow self-contained.

\paragraph{RepVGGBlock.} A standard-convolution block combining a $3 \times 3$
convolution, a $1 \times 1$ convolution, and an identity path, each followed by
batch normalization. It is used for the detail stream, where full channel mixing
is required.

\paragraph{RepDepthwiseBlock.} A depthwise block combining $3 \times 3$,
$1 \times 5$, and $5 \times 1$ depthwise convolutions with an identity path,
followed by a pointwise convolution. The two anisotropic kernels\rev{, which
extend the asymmetric convolution blocks of ACNet~\cite{ding2019acnet},} are chosen for
the elongated geometry of road segments, so the block can learn horizontal and
vertical responses separately rather than approximating both with one square
kernel. It is used wherever refinement follows a fusion step, in the decoder and
at the output of the controlled fusion module.

Fusion into a single convolution proceeds in two stages. First, a convolution
with weight $\mathbf{W}$ and bias $\mathbf{b}$ followed by batch normalization
with running mean $\mu_c$, running variance $v_c$, scale $\beta_c$, shift
$\delta_c$, and stability constant $\epsilon$ is equivalent to a single
convolution whose parameters, for output channel $c$, are
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bnfold}
\mathbf{W}'_c = \frac{\beta_c}{\sqrt{v_c + \epsilon}}\,\mathbf{W}_c,
\qquad
b'_c = \delta_c +
\frac{\beta_c\,(b_c - \mu_c)}{\sqrt{v_c + \epsilon}} .
\end{equation}
%
Second, every branch is folded by Eq.~\eqref{eq:bnfold}, the resulting kernels
are zero-padded to the largest kernel size in the block, and the padded kernels
and biases are summed,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:repsum}
\mathbf{W}^{\star} = \sum_{m} \mathrm{Pad}\!\left(\mathbf{W}'_{m}\right),
\qquad
\mathbf{b}^{\star} = \sum_{m} \mathbf{b}'_{m},
\end{equation}
%
where $m$ indexes the branches, including the identity path expressed as a
centered Dirac kernel. A RepVGGBlock collapses to one $3 \times 3$ convolution
with bias and a RepDepthwiseBlock to one $5 \times 5$ depthwise convolution with
bias. The transformation is algebraic and introduces no approximation beyond
floating-point rounding, which Section~\ref{subsec:efficiency} quantifies.

\subsection{Shared encoder}
\label{subsec:encoder}

The encoder is a ResNet-34~\cite{he2016resnet} truncated after its last residual
stage and initialized from ImageNet weights~\rev{\cite{russakovsky2015imagenet}}. It returns
five feature levels rather than one: a stride-2 stem with 64 channels, a stride-4
map with 64 channels, a stride-8 map with 128 channels, a stride-16 map with 256
channels, and a stride-32 map with 512 channels. The stride-8 map is the input of
the detail stream, the stride-16 and stride-32 maps feed the semantic stream, and
the stride-2 and stride-4 maps are reserved as decoder skips.

Both branches therefore start from pretrained features. This matters for the
initialization strategy used throughout the network: several modules below begin
as identity or near-identity operators specifically so that the pretrained
statistics are not disturbed before the randomly initialized parts have learned
anything useful.

\subsection{Dual-resolution context module}
\label{subsec:dualres}

This module holds the two streams and the exchange between them.

\paragraph{Detail stream.} The stride-8 encoder map is projected to
$C_d = 96$ channels by a $1 \times 1$ convolution and refined by two RepVGG
blocks, giving $\mathbf{D}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{96 \times H/8 \times W/8}$. After
the exchange it passes through two further RepVGG blocks. It stays at stride~8
over its whole length, which is the structural commitment the design is built
around.

\paragraph{Semantic stream.} The semantic stream is anchored at the pretrained
stride-16 map, $\mathbf{S}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{256 \times H/16 \times W/16}$. The
stride-32 map is projected to $C_s = 192$ channels and serves only as the input
of the context module of Section~\ref{subsec:dappm}.

\paragraph{Bilateral exchange.} Writing $\phi_{s \to d}$ and $\phi_{d \to s}$ for
the $1 \times 1$ projections followed by bilinear resampling to the target
resolution, the two residual terms are
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deltas}
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{s \to d} = \phi_{s \to d}(\mathbf{S}_0),
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{d \to s} = \phi_{d \to s}(\mathbf{D}_0).
\end{equation}
%
Each term is modulated by a spatial gate $\mathbf{G}$ and weighted by a
per-channel scale before it is added,
%
\begin{align}
\label{eq:exchange-detail}
\mathbf{D}_1 &= \mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(
  \mathbf{D}_0 + \boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s \to d} \odot
  \mathbf{G}_{s \to d} \odot \boldsymbol{\Delta}_{s \to d}
\right), \\
\label{eq:exchange-semantic}
\mathbf{S}_1 &= \mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(
  \mathbf{S}_0 + \boldsymbol{\gamma}_{d \to s} \odot
  \mathbf{G}_{d \to s} \odot \boldsymbol{\Delta}_{d \to s}
\right),
\end{align}
%
where $\odot$ denotes broadcast elementwise multiplication,
$\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s \to d} \in \mathbb{R}^{96}$, and
$\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{d \to s} \in \mathbb{R}^{256}$. The scales are initialized
asymmetrically: every entry of $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s \to d}$ starts at $0.10$,
so semantics assist the detail stream weakly from the first step, whereas
$\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{d \to s}$ starts at zero, so the randomly initialized detail
stream cannot perturb the pretrained semantic features before it carries useful
information. Both remain free to grow or change sign during training, and their
magnitudes are logged each epoch as a diagnostic of which route the network
actually uses.

\subsection{Residual spatial gate}
\label{subsec:gate}

Each direction of the exchange is filtered by a gate that decides \emph{where},
not by how much on average, the residual term should act. \rev{It follows the
attention gates of Schlemper et al.~\cite{schlemper2019attention}, which learn to
suppress irrelevant regions and highlight salient ones when inserted into
architectures such as U-Net; here the gate modulates a residual term exchanged
between two streams.} The gate $g$ is a small
convolutional block that receives the independently group-normalized target
stream $\mathbf{T}$ and residual term $\boldsymbol{\Delta}$, and returns a
single-channel map at the target resolution $h \times w$,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gate}
\mathbf{G} = 2\,\sigma\!\left(
  g\!\left(\mathrm{GN}(\mathbf{T}),\, \mathrm{GN}(\boldsymbol{\Delta})\right)
\right)
\in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times h \times w},
\end{equation}
%
where $\sigma$ is the logistic function and $\mathrm{GN}$ denotes group
normalization~\rev{\cite{wu2020groupnorm}}. Internally $g$ concatenates the two
normalized inputs, applies a $3 \times 3$ convolution with group normalization
and a SiLU activation~\rev{\cite{elfwing2018silu}}, and reduces to one channel with a $1 \times 1$
convolution.

Three properties of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate} are deliberate. The final convolution is
initialized to zero, so $\mathbf{G} \equiv 1$ at the first step and the gated
network is numerically identical to an ungated one; gating is therefore an exact
extension of the simpler design rather than a different starting point, and the
pretrained feature distribution is left untouched. The factor of two places unit
gain in the interior of the range instead of at a saturated endpoint, so the gate
can amplify as well as suppress. The output has a single channel because the
decision the exchange has to make, whether a location is road-like, is primarily
spatial; channel selectivity is already expressed by the per-channel scales
$\boldsymbol{\gamma}$ in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:exchange-detail}
and~\eqref{eq:exchange-semantic}, so a full channel attention map\rev{, as in
squeeze-and-excitation blocks~\cite{hu2020senet},} would add
parameters without addressing the dominant factor.

\subsection{Progressive deep aggregation pyramid pooling}
\label{subsec:dappm}

Context is aggregated at stride~32 by a progressive variant of deep aggregation
pyramid pooling~\cite{pan2023ddrnet}. Let $\mathbf{X}$ be the projected stride-32
map and let the pooling grids be traversed from fine to coarse,
$k \in (8, 4, 2, 1)$. With $\mathbf{U}_0 = \psi_0(\mathbf{X})$ the output of a
$1 \times 1$ projection, each stage is
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dappm}
\mathbf{U}_k = \pi_k\!\left(
  \mathbf{U}_{k^{-}} +
  \mathrm{Up}\!\left(\psi_k\!\left(\mathrm{AvgPool}_{k \times k}
  (\mathbf{X})\right)\right)
\right),
\end{equation}
%
where $\psi_k$ is a $1 \times 1$ projection, $\mathrm{Up}$ bilinear resampling to
the resolution of $\mathbf{X}$, $\pi_k$ a $3 \times 3$ convolution, and $k^{-}$
the preceding grid in the traversal. Each stage adds a broader context scale to
the accumulated representation \emph{before} processing it, in contrast to
pyramid pooling~\cite{zhao2017pspnet} and atrous spatial pyramid
pooling~\rev{\cite{chen2018deeplab}}, whose branches are computed independently
and combined only at the end. All stages are concatenated, compressed by a
$1 \times 1$ convolution, and added to a projected shortcut of $\mathbf{X}$.

Every convolution in this module uses group normalization rather than batch
normalization. The coarsest branch pools to a $1 \times 1$ grid, where batch
statistics are estimated from a single spatial location per sample and are
unreliable at the per-device batch size of two that 1024-pixel crops impose on a
single device.

The context returns to the semantic stream as a third gated residual,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:context}
\mathbf{S}_2 = \mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(
  \mathbf{S}_1 + \boldsymbol{\gamma}_c \odot \mathrm{Up}(\rho(\mathbf{U}))
\right),
\end{equation}
%
with $\rho$ a $1 \times 1$ projection to the semantic channel width and
$\boldsymbol{\gamma}_c \in \mathbb{R}^{256}$ initialized to $0.10$ in every
entry.

\subsection{Controlled road fusion}
\label{subsec:fusion}

The semantic stream is projected to the detail width and resampled to stride~8,
producing $\tilde{\mathbf{S}}$. Summing the two streams elementwise would force
unrelated channel identities into correspondence, so the module instead
normalizes them separately, concatenates along the channel axis, and compresses
the result,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fusion}
\mathbf{M} = \omega\!\left(
  \left[\mathrm{BN}_d(\mathbf{D}_2);\,
        \mathrm{BN}_s(\tilde{\mathbf{S}})\right]
\right),
\qquad
\mathbf{D}_3 = \mathcal{R}\!\left(
  \mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(\mathbf{D}_2 +
  \boldsymbol{\gamma}_f \odot \mathbf{M}\right)
\right),
\end{equation}
%
where $[\cdot;\cdot]$ is channel concatenation, $\omega$ a $1 \times 1$
convolution back to $C_d$ channels, $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_f \in \mathbb{R}^{96}$ a
per-channel scale initialized to $0.10$, and $\mathcal{R}$ a RepDepthwiseBlock
that refines the fused road geometry. The detail stream remains the residual
anchor, so semantic evidence enters as a correction whose magnitude the network
controls channel by channel.

\subsection{Road reconstruction decoder and centerline head}
\label{subsec:decoder}

The decoder returns the fused stride-8 representation to the input resolution in
three steps. The fused map is projected to 64 channels and upsampled to stride~4,
concatenated with a 32-channel projection of the stride-4 encoder map, fused back
to 64 channels, and refined by two RepDepthwiseBlocks. The same pattern repeats
at stride~2 against a 16-channel projection of the stem, with a fused width of 32
channels. The result is resampled to the exact input size, passed through a
separable convolution with 24 channels and dropout at rate 0.05, and mapped to
two logits by a $1 \times 1$ convolution.

An auxiliary head at stride~4 predicts a single-channel centerline map from the
64-channel stride-4 representation, using a $3 \times 3$ convolution to 32
channels followed by a $1 \times 1$ convolution. The head is evaluated only when
the module is in training mode, so inference returns a single logits tensor and
the auxiliary branch adds no inference compute. Its 18\,529 parameters remain in
the checkpoint but are never executed at deployment.

\subsection{Training objective}
\label{subsec:loss}

The objective combines a class-balanced pixel term, a region term, and a
connectivity term,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loss}
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}
+ \lambda_{\mathrm{dice}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dice}}
+ \lambda_{\mathrm{aux}}(t)\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cl}},
\end{equation}
%
where $t$ is the epoch index. The three terms are defined below.

\paragraph{Class-balanced cross-entropy.} $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}$ is the
two-class cross-entropy with a weight of one on background and $w$ on the road
class. The weight is derived from the training-set imbalance rather than tuned.
Let $n_1$ be the number of road pixels and $n_0$ the number of background pixels
over the training split; then
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:roadweight}
r = \frac{n_0}{n_1},
\qquad
w = \min\!\left(\sqrt{r},\, w_{\max}\right),
\end{equation}
%
with $w_{\max} = 2.0$. The square root damps the raw ratio, which reaches one to
two orders of magnitude, and the cap prevents the objective from being dominated
by recall on datasets with especially sparse labels.

\paragraph{Region term.} $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dice}}$ is the soft Dice
loss~\cite{milletari2016vnet} computed on the road probability map of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:threshold} against the binary label, which stabilizes gradients
when the positive class is rare. Its weight is $\lambda_{\mathrm{dice}} = 1$.

\paragraph{Centerline term.} The connectivity term supervises the auxiliary head
of Section~\ref{subsec:decoder} against a centerline target derived from the
label without any external morphological library. Erosion and dilation on a soft
mask $\mathbf{A}$ are expressed through max-pooling\rev{, as in the
soft skeletonization of clDice~\cite{shit2021cldice}},
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:morph}
\mathrm{er}(\mathbf{A}) = -\mathrm{maxpool}_{3\times3}(-\mathbf{A}),
\qquad
\mathrm{di}(\mathbf{A}) = \mathrm{maxpool}_{3\times3}(\mathbf{A}),
\end{equation}
%
and the opening is $\mathrm{op}(\mathbf{A}) =
\mathrm{di}(\mathrm{er}(\mathbf{A}))$. The skeleton accumulates the residual
between each eroded mask and its opening over $n_{\mathrm{it}}$ iterations,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:skeleton}
\mathbf{K} = \sum_{i=0}^{n_{\mathrm{it}}}
\mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(
  \mathbf{A}_i - \mathrm{op}(\mathbf{A}_i)
\right),
\qquad
\mathbf{A}_{i+1} = \mathrm{er}(\mathbf{A}_i),
\end{equation}
%
with $\mathbf{A}_0 = \mathbf{Y}$ and $n_{\mathrm{it}} = 8$. Because the whole
construction uses only pooling operations, it runs on the accelerator inside the
training graph and introduces no dependency on an external morphology library.
The skeleton is computed at the label resolution and only then dilated by one
pixel and reduced to stride~4 by adaptive max-pooling. The reverse order,
downsampling before thinning, merges adjacent branches and intersections, which
is exactly the structure the term is meant to supervise.

The centerline head is trained with the Tversky loss~\cite{salehi2017tversky}.
Writing $\hat{k}_j$ for the predicted centerline probability at location $j$ and
$k_j$ for the target,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tversky}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cl}} = 1 -
\frac{\sum_j \hat{k}_j k_j + \varepsilon}
     {\sum_j \hat{k}_j k_j
      + \alpha \sum_j \hat{k}_j (1 - k_j)
      + \beta \sum_j (1 - \hat{k}_j) k_j + \varepsilon},
\end{equation}
%
with $\alpha = 0.30$, $\beta = 0.70$, and $\varepsilon$ a small constant. Since
$\beta > \alpha$, a missed centerline pixel costs more than a spurious one, which
matches the operational asymmetry of the task: a break severs a route, whereas an
extra centerline pixel does not.

The auxiliary weight follows a ramp,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ramp}
\lambda_{\mathrm{aux}}(t) =
\lambda_{\mathrm{aux}}^{\max}
\min\!\left(
  1, \frac{\max(0,\, t - t_0)}{t_w}
\right),
\end{equation}
%
with $\lambda_{\mathrm{aux}}^{\max} = 0.15$, start epoch $t_0 = 5$, and ramp
length $t_w = 5$. Supervising a centerline before the coarse mask is
approximately correct would provide a target the model cannot yet use.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:experimental-setup}
To demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed architecture, we evaluate its performance on public road segmentation datasets, detailing the experimental setup, implementation specifics, and a comparative analysis against state-of-the-art methods.

\subsection{Datasets and splits}
\label{subsec:datasets}

We benchmark DBR-Net on two public road extraction datasets:

- The Massachusetts Roads dataset \cite{mnih2013aerial} comprises 1,171 aerial images of Massachusetts, each with a spatial resolution of 1 m/pixel and a size of $1500 \times 1500$ pixels. Covering more than 2,600 km$^2$, the dataset is officially divided into 1,108 training, 14 validation, and 49 test images. The corresponding ground-truth road masks were derived from OpenStreetMap centerlines and rasterized using a line width of 7 pixels.

- The DeepGlobe Road Extraction dataset \cite{demir2018deepglobe} consists of 8,570 RGB satellite images acquired over diverse urban and rural regions in Thailand, Indonesia, and India. Each image has a size of $1024 \times 1024$ pixels and a spatial resolution of 0.5 m/pixel. Covering approximately 2,220 km$^2$, the dataset is divided into 6,226 training, 1,243 validation, and 1,101 test images. The ground-truth masks provide pixel-level binary annotations of the complete road surfaces rather than road centerlines, with road and background pixels represented as foreground and background classes, respectively.

\subsection{Implementation details}
\label{subsec:implementation}

All experiments were conducted on a workstation running 64-bit Windows 11 Pro,
equipped with two Intel Xeon Gold 6138 CPUs operating at 2.0~GHz and an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU with 24~GB of memory. The implementation environment comprised Python 3.10.13, PyTorch 3.11.6, CUDA Toolkit 12.2, and cuDNN 8.9.

The encoder is a ResNet-34 \cite{he2016resnet} initialized from ImageNet weights \cite{russakovsky2015imagenet}. The detail stream carries 96 channels, the semantic projection 192 channels, and the context module 32 channels per branch. Training
uses $1024 \times 1024$ crops with a per-device batch size of two and two
gradient accumulation steps, giving an effective batch size of four on a single device.

Optimization uses AdamW \cite{loshchilov2019adamw} with decoupled weight decay of $1\times10^{-4}$ applied only to tensors with more than one dimension, so that
biases, normalization parameters, and gate scales are excluded. Learning rates are layered relative to a base rate of $\eta = 2\times10^{-4}$: the decoder uses
 the full rate, the dual-resolution module a factor of 0.75, the stride-16 and
 stride-32 encoder stages together with the stride-8 stage a factor of 0.20, and
 the stem with the first residual stage a factor of 0.10. The schedule is a linear
 warmup over five epochs starting from a factor of 0.10, followed by cosine
 annealing to 2\% of the base rate, stepped once per optimizer update rather than
 once per epoch. Gradients are clipped to a norm of 3.0. Mixed-precision training
 in float16 with dynamic loss scaling is used throughout; when the scaler skips an
 update because of non-finite gradients, the scheduler and the weight average skip
 the same update, which keeps them synchronized. An exponential moving average of
 the weights with decay 0.999 and warmup ramp $d_t = 0.999\,(1 - e^{-t/2000})$ is maintained, and the averaged weights are the
ones validated, tested, and reported. In its training-time form, the proposed DBR-Net contains 22,541,845 trainable parameters.

Runs use a fixed seed of 42 for model initialization and data order, and 3407 for split generation.
Data augmentation comprised random horizontal and vertical flips, each applied with a probability of 0.5; random rotations by $90^\circ$, $180^\circ$, or $270^\circ$, applied with a probability of 0.25; and random scaling with a scale factor sampled from $[0.75, 1.0]$..

\subsection{Evaluation protocol and metrics}
\label{subsec:metrics}

Images are never resized for evaluation. A sliding window of $1024 \times 1024$
with an overlap of 256 pixels, giving a stride of 768, traverses each scene, and
tiles smaller than the window are reflect-padded. Each tile is weighted by a
separable two-dimensional Hann window clamped below at 0.05 and accumulated in
the logit domain; the accumulated logits are divided by the accumulated weights,
which removes tile seams. Writing $\mathbf{W}^{(p)}$ for the window at position
$p$ and $\mathbf{Z}^{(p)}$ for the tile logits placed in scene coordinates,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:blend}
\mathbf{Z} = \frac{\sum_p \mathbf{W}^{(p)} \odot \mathbf{Z}^{(p)}}
                  {\sum_p \mathbf{W}^{(p)}} .
\end{equation}

Metrics are computed from a confusion matrix pooled over the whole split. Let
$\mathrm{TP}$, $\mathrm{FP}$, and $\mathrm{FN}$ count road-class true positives,
false positives, and false negatives. The primary metric is the road-class
intersection-over-union,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:iou}
\mathrm{IoU}_{\mathrm{road}} =
\frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP} + \mathrm{FP} + \mathrm{FN}},
\end{equation}
%
reported as a micro-average over pooled counts. Background IoU, mean IoU,
precision, recall, and F1 follow from the same pooled matrix, and a macro variant
of road IoU averaged over images is reported alongside the micro value because
the two diverge when road density varies strongly across scenes.

Route quality is quantified by a relaxed F1 score with a tolerance of three
pixels, which corresponds to three meters on Massachusetts imagery and 1.5 meters
on DeepGlobe imagery. Let $\mathcal{D}_b(\cdot)$ denote binary dilation by $b$
pixels. Relaxed precision counts predicted road pixels that fall inside the
dilated label, relaxed recall counts label pixels that fall inside the dilated
prediction\rev{, following the relaxed precision and recall of
Mnih~\cite{mnih2013aerial}}, and the relaxed F1 is their harmonic mean,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:relaxedf1}
\mathrm{P}_{b} =
\frac{\left|\hat{\mathbf{Y}} \cap \mathcal{D}_b(\mathbf{Y})\right|}
     {\left|\hat{\mathbf{Y}}\right|},
\qquad
\mathrm{R}_{b} =
\frac{\left|\mathbf{Y} \cap \mathcal{D}_b(\hat{\mathbf{Y}})\right|}
     {\left|\mathbf{Y}\right|} .
\end{equation}
%
The tolerance absorbs the one- to two-pixel jitter of hand-drawn road labels, so
the score reflects whether a route was found rather than whether its boundary was
placed exactly.

Two thresholds are reported for every metric. The fixed setting uses
$\tau = 0.5$. The calibrated setting sweeps $\tau$ from 0.20 to 0.80 in steps of
0.02 and selects the value maximizing road IoU, using a 1001-bin histogram of
predicted probabilities per true class so that full probability maps never have
to be stored. The sweep is permitted on validation splits only; the selected
threshold is then applied unchanged to the test split.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

Several limitations bound the scope of these results. The model predicts raster
masks and does not extract a road graph, so vectorization, node and edge
recovery, and downstream routing are outside the evaluated pipeline\rev{, whereas
graph-based methods such as RNGDet~\cite{xu2022rngdet} produce the
graph directly and recent work increasingly shifts from raster to vector
extraction~\cite{luweng2025roadreview}}. Roads are
treated as a single class, with no distinction between highway, urban, and
unpaved surfaces. The input is restricted to three-band optical imagery, so
multispectral, synthetic aperture radar, and time-series inputs are untested. On
DeepGlobe, the validation split is a subset of the test split, as stated in
Section~\ref{subsec:datasets}, so DeepGlobe test scores carry a model-selection
dependency that Massachusetts scores do not. Both benchmarks cover a limited
range of geographies and acquisition conditions, and generalization to regions or
sensors not represented in them is not established by these experiments.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper addressed road extraction from high-resolution optical imagery under
the three constraints that distinguish it from general segmentation: severe class
imbalance, structures too thin to survive deep downsampling, and a quality
criterion that depends on connectivity rather than on area. DBR-Net
responds with a detail stream held at stride~8 for its entire length, a semantic
stream that gathers context at stride~32 without being asked to preserve thin
structures, and a single bilateral exchange between them regulated by a
zero-initialized spatial gate that begins as an identity and learns selectivity
during training. Connectivity is supervised by an auxiliary centerline head whose
targets are produced by max-pooling-based thinning applied before downsampling
and optimized with an asymmetric Tversky loss.

Under a protocol that evaluates at native resolution with Hann-blended
overlapping tiles and calibrates decision thresholds on validation data only, the
model reaches a road IoU of [IOU\_MASS] on the Massachusetts test split and
[IOU\_DG] on the DeepGlobe test split. [CONCLUSION SENTENCE:
summarize which component the ablation identifies as the principal contributor,
and to which metric.] After re-parameterization the deployed network is a
single-path model of 22.50~M parameters and 90.46~GMACs per $1024\times1024$
tile, and the fusion is exact to float32 round-off.

The raster-mask formulation is the natural boundary of this work. Extending the
centerline head from a supervision signal into an explicit graph extraction
stage, and testing whether the gated exchange transfers to sensors other than
three-band optical imagery, are the directions we consider most likely to be
productive.

\begin{abstract}
Automatic road extraction from high-resolution satellite and aerial imagery
supports map updating, logistics planning, and post-disaster response. The task
remains difficult because road surfaces cover only a few percent of the pixels,
form thin and elongated structures that are frequently occluded by trees,
shadows, and vehicles, and are useful only when their connectivity is preserved.
Encoder-decoder networks that downsample deeply discard the evidence for narrow
roads before the decoder can recover it, whereas networks that hold high
resolution throughout are costly at inference time. This paper presents
DBR-Net (Dual-Branch Road Network), a segmentation network built around a detail
stream that never leaves stride~8 and a semantic stream that descends to
stride~32 to gather context. The two streams communicate through a single
bilateral exchange.
Road context is aggregated by a progressive pyramid pooling module built on
group normalization and re-enters the detail stream through a controlled fusion
step. Connectivity is supervised by an auxiliary centerline head trained with an
asymmetric objective whose targets come from a pure-tensor morphological
thinning applied before any downsampling. Every multi-branch block fuses exactly
into a single convolution for deployment. On the Massachusetts Roads and
DeepGlobe benchmarks, the proposed network reaches a road IoU of
72.19\% and 61.48\%, respectively, exceeding the strongest baseline from 14 road extracting methods with higher speed and less computational cost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Road networks form a fundamental layer of geospatial information systems and
support applications such as map updating, logistics planning, and emergency
response. However, road maps can become outdated as construction, closures, and
damage alter the network faster than manual digitization can capture. The
increasing availability of high-resolution optical satellite and aerial imagery
makes automatic road extraction a practical means of updating this information
\rev{\cite{abdollahi2020roadreview,chen2022roadsurvey,luweng2025roadreview}}.
For many downstream applications, accurate road coverage is important, but so is
the continuity of the extracted network because even a short missing segment can
interrupt an otherwise valid route.

Road extraction is commonly formulated as binary semantic segmentation, but it
remains challenging for several reasons. Road pixels often occupy only a small
fraction of an image, which can bias pixel-wise learning toward the background
class. Roads are also thin, elongated structures whose visual evidence may be
weakened by repeated downsampling. Their appearance varies with surface material,
illumination, and acquisition conditions, while rivers, field boundaries,
rooftops, and other linear structures can produce similar local patterns.
Occlusion by trees, shadows, buildings, and vehicles introduces additional gaps
\rev{\cite{mnih2013aerial,cheng2017cascaded,zhou2020btroadnet}}. Consequently,
effective road extraction requires both fine spatial detail and broader semantic
context. Pixel-overlap metrics remain useful for evaluation, but they may
under-penalize small disconnections that are important to network usability.

Existing approaches address these challenges along two main directions.
Encoder--decoder models, including U-Net \cite{ronneberger2015unet}, Deep
Residual U-Net \cite{zhang2018resunet}, and D-LinkNet
\cite{zhou2018dlinknet}, recover spatial resolution through skip connections and
decoder stages. Later methods improve contextual representation through
non-local operations \cite{wang2022nllinknet}, multi-resolution exchange
\cite{wang2021hrnet}, or attention-based decoders
\cite{xie2021segformer,wang2022unetformer}. A complementary line of research
introduces geometric or connectivity-related supervision, including road
centerlines \cite{cheng2017cascaded,lu2019msmtre}, local direction
\cite{ding2021diresnet}, connectivity attention \cite{mei2021coanet}, and
topology-aware objectives \cite{oner2022connectivity,shit2021cldice}. Although
these developments improve road delineation, retaining high-resolution features
throughout a network can increase computation, whereas aggressive downsampling
can remove evidence for narrow roads. Multi-branch feature aggregation may also
improve optimization while adding inference-time cost. These trade-offs motivate
a design that preserves spatial detail selectively, incorporates contextual
information, and remains efficient after deployment.

To this end, DBR-Net is designed as an asymmetric dual-branch network for road
extraction. A detail stream remains at stride~8 to retain fine spatial evidence,
while a semantic stream reaches stride~32 to capture broader context. The two
streams communicate through a single bilateral exchange controlled by a
single-channel spatial gate. The final g ate convolution is zero-initialized so
that the exchange initially behaves likeits ungated residual counterpart and
learns spatial selectivity during training. A progressive pyramid pooling module
aggregates multi-scale context, and a controlled fusion module returns this
information to the detail stream. In addition, an auxiliary centerline head
encourages structural continuity. Its targets are generated before downsampling
using max-pooling-based morphological thinning, and the head is optimized with
an asymmetric Tversky loss that assigns a larger penalty to false-negative road
pixels than to false positives \cite{salehi2017tversky}. The multi-branch blocks
are structurally re-parameterized into single convolutions for deployment
\cite{ding2021repvgg}.

DBR-Net is evaluated on the Massachusetts Roads \cite{mnih2013aerial} and
DeepGlobe \cite{demir2018deepglobe} datasets. DBR-Net achieves road IoU scores
of 72.19\% and 61.76\%, respectively, exceeding the strongest compared baseline
by 0.75 percentage points on both benchmarks. The corresponding F1-scores are
84.02\% and 74.21\%. For a $512\times512$ input, the model contains 22.53~M
parameters, requires 90.457~GFLOPs, and uses 3.99~GB of GPU memory.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
\item A dual-branch architecture is designed to preserve global context and local context
      through a single gated bilateral exchange.
\item An auxiliary centerline-supervision is  whose targets are
      generated by tensor-based morphological thinning before downsampling and
      optimized with an asymmetric ClDice objective to emphasize missed road
      pixels.
\item Re-parameterizable blocks and demonstrate a favorable accuracy--efficiency
      balance are combined on two public road-extraction benchmarks.
\end{enumerate}


\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Encoder-decoder networks for road extraction}
\label{subsec:rw-encdec}

Mnih~\cite{mnih2013aerial} trained neural networks on large aerial image
datasets to label road pixels from a wide image context, because occlusions and
shadows cast by trees and tall buildings often make local cues insufficient,
and released the Massachusetts Roads dataset, which established patch-based
learning of road masks from aerial imagery as a benchmark task. Fully
convolutional networks~\cite{shelhamer2017fcn}, trained end to end from pixels
to pixels, then moved segmentation from patch-wise classification to dense
prediction over inputs of arbitrary size. U-Net
\cite{ronneberger2015unet} introduced the skip-connected encoder-decoder that
most later road models inherit: the encoder builds semantic capacity by
downsampling, and lateral skips return spatial detail to the decoder. Deep
Residual U-Net \cite{zhang2018resunet} rebuilt this architecture from residual
units and reported improved road delineation on Massachusetts with fewer
parameters than the original.

The DeepGlobe challenge \cite{demir2018deepglobe} shifted attention to
0.5\,m/pixel satellite imagery and larger scene variety. Its winning entry,
D-LinkNet \cite{zhou2018dlinknet}, built on the LinkNet architecture with a
pretrained encoder, inserted a cascade of dilated convolutions at
the encoder bottleneck, enlarging the receptive field without an additional
downsampling stage, and it remains the reference baseline on that benchmark.
NL-LinkNet \cite{wang2022nllinknet} added non-local blocks to a LinkNet-based
network, so that every location can draw on global context, and outperformed
D-LinkNet with 43\% fewer parameters. The
shared limitation of this family is structural rather than incidental. Skip
connections return detail that survived the encoder, but a road eight to twenty
pixels wide is represented by a fraction of a pixel at $1/32$ resolution, so what the
decoder receives through the deepest skips is context, not geometry. Dilation
mitigates the problem by avoiding one downsampling step; it does not remove the
dependence on a deeply downsampled trunk.

\subsection{Multi-resolution and dual-branch architectures}
\label{subsec:rw-multires}

A second design maintains high resolution throughout the network instead of
recovering it. HRNet \cite{wang2021hrnet} runs parallel branches at several
resolutions and repeatedly exchanges information among all of them, which keeps a
high-resolution representation available at every depth. The approach is
effective for position-sensitive tasks, but the exchange is dense: every pair of
resolutions is fused at every stage, and the cost grows with the number of
branches.

Real-time segmentation networks reduce that cost to two branches. BiSeNet~V2
\cite{yu2021bisenetv2} pairs a shallow detail branch with a deeper semantic
branch and merges them once through a guided aggregation layer. DDRNet
\cite{pan2023ddrnet} keeps two resolutions throughout and exchanges between them
repeatedly, and introduces the deep aggregation pyramid pooling module to enlarge
the receptive field at the low-resolution branch. Both were developed for
real-time scene parsing and benchmarked chiefly on street-level scenes, where
objects are large and the detail branch mainly sharpens boundaries. Overhead
road imagery inverts that balance: the structures of interest are themselves
near the resolution limit, so which resolutions exchange, and under what
control, matters more than how many exchanges occur. DDRNet sums projected
features of the two branches without any gating, and the guided aggregation
layer of BiSeNet~V2 multiplies each branch by sigmoid-activated responses of
the other, a dense gate over every channel and location rather than a single
spatial map; neither is initialized so that adding the exchange leaves a
pretrained encoder undisturbed. Within road extraction, DBRANet
\cite{chen2022dbranet} places a dual-branch module, a residual block beside a
refined asymmetric block, in the encoder of a U-shaped network and decodes
with a regional attention module; its two branches strengthen the feature
extraction of the encoder, and the network keeps the encoder-decoder layout
discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:rw-encdec}.

Context aggregation modules developed for scene parsing are used across both
families. Pyramid pooling \cite{zhao2017pspnet} and atrous spatial pyramid
pooling \cite{chen2018deeplab} both compute several receptive-field scales
in parallel and combine them once at the end. The deep aggregation variant
\cite{pan2023ddrnet} adds hierarchical residual connections among the pooling
branches. These modules are normally built on batch normalization, which becomes
unreliable at the small per-device batch sizes \cite{wu2020groupnorm} that
1024-pixel remote sensing crops impose, particularly for a branch that pools
to a single spatial location.



\subsection{Structural re-parameterization}
\label{subsec:rw-reparam}

Multi-branch blocks improve optimization but cost inference time. ACNet
\cite{ding2019acnet} strengthened square kernels with parallel
one-dimensional asymmetric convolutions during training and converted the
trained network back into its original architecture, so the added branches
cost nothing at inference. RepVGG
\cite{ding2021repvgg} showed that a training-time block of parallel $3\times3$,
$1\times1$, and identity paths, each followed by batch normalization, can be
folded algebraically into a single $3\times3$ convolution, so the deployed model
is a plain sequential stack with no accuracy loss. The transformation is exact:
batch normalization folds into the preceding convolution, and kernels padded to a
common size sum linearly.

Re-parameterization has been applied mainly to classification backbones
\cite{ding2019acnet, ding2021repvgg} and
general-purpose detectors. In the present network, the detail stages use the
standard ungrouped RepVGG formulation, whereas the post-fusion and decoder
refinement stages use a depthwise variant with $3\times3$, $1\times5$, and
$5\times1$ spatial branches followed by pointwise channel mixing, following
the same fold-and-sum algebra as ACNet's asymmetric branches. The
anisotropic branches are motivated by the elongated geometry of road
segments, in the spirit of the strip convolutions of CoANet
\cite{mei2021coanet}, and fuse into a single $5\times5$ depthwise kernel at
deployment.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Problem formulation and notation}
\label{subsec:formulation}

Let $\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times H \times W}$ denote an optical RGB image
of height $H$ and width $W$, and let
$\mathbf{Y} \in \{0,1\}^{H \times W}$ denote the corresponding label, where
$Y_{ij} = 1$ marks a road-surface pixel. The task is to learn a mapping
$f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ that produces two-channel logits at the input
resolution,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:task}
\mathbf{Z} = f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{I}) \in
\mathbb{R}^{2 \times H \times W},
\end{equation}
%
where $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ collects all trainable parameters. The road
probability map $\mathbf{P} \in [0,1]^{H \times W}$ and the predicted mask
$\hat{\mathbf{Y}}$ follow from the channel-wise softmax and a decision threshold
$\tau$,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:threshold}
P_{ij} = \frac{\exp(Z_{1ij})}{\exp(Z_{0ij}) + \exp(Z_{1ij})},
\qquad
\hat{Y}_{ij} = \mathbb{1}\!\left[P_{ij} \ge \tau\right],
\end{equation}
%
with $\mathbb{1}[\cdot]$ the indicator function. The default threshold is
$\tau = 0.5$; Section~\ref{subsec:metrics} describes how a calibrated threshold
is estimated on validation data only.

Feature tensors are written $\mathbf{F}^{(s)}
\in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H/s \times W/s}$, where the superscript is the output
stride $s$ relative to the input. Scalars are italic lowercase, vectors bold
lowercase, and tensors bold uppercase. Throughout the paper, $\mathbf{D}$ denotes
the detail stream and $\mathbf{S}$ the semantic stream. In prose, resolution
is written as a fraction of the input (e.g., $1/8$ resolution); in tables
and figure labels, where space is limited, S$s$ abbreviates the same
quantity (e.g., S8 for $1/8$ resolution). This shorthand is unrelated to the
semantic-stream tensor $\mathbf{S}$ and its indexed instances
$\mathbf{S}_0,\mathbf{S}_1$.

\subsection{Overall architecture}
\label{subsec:overview}

\begin{figure*}[pos=t]
    \centering
    \placeholderfig[0.95\linewidth]{graph.png}
    \caption{Overall architecture of the proposed DBR-Net.}
    \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} summarizes the overall architecture: a shared
ResNet-34 encoder, the gated dual-branch pathways, progressive DAPPM
semantic context, and road-aware fusion detailed in
Section~\ref{subsec:dualres}, and the reconstruction decoder described
below.

The encoder is a ResNet-34~\cite{he2016resnet} truncated after its last
residual stage and initialized from ImageNet weights~\cite{deng2009imagenet},
following the standard ResNet-34 stage layout with 3, 4, 6, and 3 basic
blocks across its four residual stages, each combining convolution, batch
normalization, and ReLU. It returns five feature levels rather than one: a
$1/2$-resolution stem with 64 channels (E1), a $1/4$-resolution map with 64
channels (E2), a $1/8$-resolution map with 128 channels (E3), a
$1/16$-resolution map with 256 channels (E4), and a $1/32$-resolution map
with 512 channels (E5). E3 initializes the detail branch, the
E4 and E5 maps feed the semantic branch, and E1 and
E2 maps are reserved as decoder skips.

The detail branch is refined in two stages separated by a one-way
semantic-to-detail residual that injects evidence from the pretrained
E4 feature; the second stage is followed by the strip-pooling
module, which adds long-range horizontal and vertical context without
reducing the E3 resolution. The semantic branch keeps the pretrained
E4 feature as its anchor while the E5 feature is processed by
the progressive DAPPM to aggregate multi-scale context; the resulting
representation is projected, upsampled, and added residually to the
E4 anchor before the road-aware fusion stage combines the two
branches.

\label{subsec:decoder}
The decoder recovers the input resolution in three steps. $\mathbf{F}_{f}$
is first projected from 96 to 64 channels and upsampled from $1/8$ to
$1/4$ resolution, concatenated with the projected $1/4$-resolution encoder
feature (E2), fused back to 64 channels by a $1\times1$ convolution, and
refined by two RepDepthwiseBlocks. The result is upsampled to $1/2$
resolution, concatenated with the projected $1/2$-resolution stem feature
(E1), fused to 32 channels, and refined by two more RepDepthwiseBlocks.
Finally, the representation is bilinearly resized to the input resolution,
processed by a separable convolution to 24 channels, and mapped to the
two road/background logits by a $1\times1$ classifier after dropout with
rate 0.05. There is no centerline or topology-prediction branch, so at
inference the network returns only this full-resolution prediction.

\subsection{Re-parameterizable building blocks}
\label{subsec:reparam}

The refinement stages of every module are built from two block types that are
multi-branch during training and single-branch at inference, following the
re-parameterization principle of RepVGG~\cite{ding2021repvgg}. Introducing them
first makes the module descriptions that follow self-contained.

\begin{figure*}[pos=t]
    \centering
    \placeholderfig[0.8\linewidth]{repvgg.png}
    \caption{RepVGGBlock and RepDepthwiseBlock in their training-time
    multi-branch form and their algebraically equivalent deployment-time
    fused form.}
    \label{fig:reparam}
\end{figure*}

Fig.~\ref{fig:reparam} illustrates both block types before and after this
fusion.

The RepVGGBlock is a standard-convolution block combining a $3 \times 3$
convolution, a $1 \times 1$ convolution, and an identity path, each followed by
batch normalization. It is used for the detail stream, where full channel mixing
is required. The RepDepthwiseBlock is a depthwise block combining $3 \times 3$,
$1 \times 5$, and $5 \times 1$ depthwise convolutions with an identity path,
followed by a pointwise convolution. The two anisotropic kernels are chosen for
the elongated geometry of road segments, so the block can learn horizontal and
vertical responses separately rather than approximating both with one square
kernel. It is used wherever refinement follows a fusion step, in the decoder and
at the output of the controlled fusion module.

Fusion into a single convolution proceeds in two stages. First, a convolution
with weight $\mathbf{W}$ and bias $\mathbf{b}$ followed by batch normalization
with running mean $\mu_c$, running variance $v_c$, scale $\beta_c$, shift
$\delta_c$, and stability constant $\epsilon$ is equivalent to a single
convolution whose parameters, for output channel $c$, are
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bnfold}
\mathbf{W}'_c = \frac{\beta_c}{\sqrt{v_c + \epsilon}}\,\mathbf{W}_c,
\qquad
b'_c = \delta_c +
\frac{\beta_c\,(b_c - \mu_c)}{\sqrt{v_c + \epsilon}} .
\end{equation}
%
Second, every branch is folded by Eq.~\eqref{eq:bnfold}, the resulting kernels
are zero-padded to the largest kernel size in the block, and the padded kernels
and biases are summed,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:repsum}
\mathbf{W}^{\star} = \sum_{m} \mathrm{Pad}\!\left(\mathbf{W}'_{m}\right),
\qquad
\mathbf{b}^{\star} = \sum_{m} \mathbf{b}'_{m},
\end{equation}
%
where $m$ indexes the branches, including the identity path expressed as a
centered Dirac kernel. A RepVGGBlock collapses to one $3 \times 3$ convolution
with bias and a RepDepthwiseBlock to one $5 \times 5$ depthwise convolution with
bias. The transformation is algebraic and introduces no approximation beyond
floating-point rounding, which Section~\ref{subsec:efficiency} quantifies.

\subsection{Dual-branch pathways and road-aware fusion}
\label{subsec:dualres}

\label{subsec:gate}
The same lightweight spatial-gating mechanism is reused at two locations
introduced below: the S16$\rightarrow$S8 semantic-to-detail guidance and the
final road-aware fusion. For a target feature $\mathbf{T}$ and an aligned
projected source $\boldsymbol{\Delta}$, the gate is
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gate}
\mathbf{G}=2\,\sigma\!\left(
g\!\left[
\mathrm{GN}(\mathbf{T});
\mathrm{GN}(\boldsymbol{\Delta})
\right]\right),
\end{equation}
%
where $g$ consists of a $3\times3$ convolution, group normalization, SiLU,
and a final $1\times1$ convolution to one channel. The final convolution is
initialized to zero, so $\mathbf{G}=2\sigma(0)=1$ at initialization.

The shared $1/8$-resolution encoder feature
$\mathbf{F}^{(8)}\in\mathbb{R}^{128\times H/8\times W/8}$ is first projected
to $C_d=96$ channels by a $1\times1$ convolution,
%
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{D}_0 =
\phi\!\left(\mathrm{BN}\!\left(
\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1}^{128\rightarrow96}
(\mathbf{F}^{(8)})\right)\right),
\end{equation}
%
where $\phi$ denotes ReLU. Two RepVGG blocks then produce
$\mathbf{D}_1\in\mathbb{R}^{96\times H/8\times W/8}$. No operation in the
detail branch changes its spatial resolution.

The pretrained $1/16$-resolution feature
$\mathbf{S}_0\in\mathbb{R}^{256\times H/16\times W/16}$ is projected to the
detail width and bilinearly upsampled,
%
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{s\rightarrow d}
=
\mathrm{Up}_{16\rightarrow8}
\left(
\phi_{s\rightarrow d}(\mathbf{S}_0)
\right),
\end{equation}
%
where $\phi_{s\rightarrow d}$ is a $1\times1$ convolution followed by batch
normalization without an activation. The residual is modulated by the
spatial gate of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate}, $\mathbf{G}_{s\rightarrow d}$, and by a
learnable per-channel scale
$\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s\rightarrow d}\in\mathbb{R}^{96}$,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exchange-detail}
\mathbf{D}_{g}=
\mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(
\mathbf{D}_1+
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s\rightarrow d}
\odot
\mathbf{G}_{s\rightarrow d}
\odot
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{s\rightarrow d}
\right).
\end{equation}
%
The scale is initialized to $0.10$. Importantly, the current architecture has
no detail-to-semantic residual.

After semantic guidance, two further RepVGG blocks refine $\mathbf{D}_{g}$
into $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{rep}}\in\mathbb{R}^{96\times H/8\times W/8}$. This
feature is passed through a lightweight residual strip-pooling block
inspired by the strip pooling strategy of Hou et al.~\cite{hou2020strip}. A
$1\times1$ convolution first reduces $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{rep}}$ to
$\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{C_r\times h\times w}$ with
$C_r=\max(16,C_d/4)=24$, from which horizontal and vertical descriptors are
computed as
%
\begin{align}
\mathbf{H} &=
\mathrm{Conv}_{3\times1}
\left(
\mathrm{AvgPool}_{(h,1)}(\mathbf{R})
\right),\\
\mathbf{V} &=
\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times3}
\left(
\mathrm{AvgPool}_{(1,w)}(\mathbf{R})
\right).
\end{align}
%
Both descriptors are group-normalized, activated, broadcast back to
$h\times w$, summed, and projected from 24 to 96 channels. The final detail
feature is
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:strippool}
\mathbf{D}_2 =
\mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{rep}}+
\psi\!\left(
\mathrm{ReLU}(\mathrm{Up}(\mathbf{H})+\mathrm{Up}(\mathbf{V}))
\right)
\right),
\end{equation}
%
where $\psi$ is a $1\times1$ projection. This module supplies directional
long-range context while preserving the $1/8$-resolution geometry.

\label{subsec:dappm}
The semantic branch gathers its own context symmetrically to the detail
branch's strip pooling above. The path starts from the pretrained
$1/32$-resolution encoder feature
$\mathbf{F}^{(32)}\in\mathbb{R}^{512\times H/32\times W/32}$. A $1\times1$
projection reduces it to 192 channels before the deep aggregation pyramid
pooling module (DAPPM) of DDRNet~\cite{pan2023ddrnet}, used here for the
semantic branch with group normalization in place of batch normalization and
adapted channel widths. The module uses an internal branch width of 32
channels and adaptive pooling grids $\{1,2,4,8\}$; following DDRNet, the
implementation traverses them as $(8,4,2,1)$ so that each scale's context is
folded into the running feature before the next, coarser scale is added,
rather than pooling every scale independently and combining once at the end.
Let $\mathbf{X}$ denote the 192-channel projected feature and $\mathbf{U}_0$
its 32-channel $1\times1$ projection. The update at each scale is
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dappm}
\mathbf{U}_{k}=
\pi_k\!\left(
\mathbf{U}_{k^{-}}+
\mathrm{Up}\!\left[
\psi_k\!\left(
\mathrm{AvgPool}_{k\times k}(\mathbf{X})
\right)\right]
\right),
\end{equation}
%
where $\psi_k$ is a $1\times1$ projection to 32 channels and $\pi_k$ is a
$3\times3$ convolution. Group normalization is used throughout the DAPPM
branches. The base projection and all progressive outputs are concatenated and
compressed back to 192 channels, then added to a 192-channel shortcut.

The DAPPM output is projected from 192 to 256 channels and bilinearly
upsampled to $1/16$ resolution. It is injected into the pretrained semantic anchor with
a learnable scale $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{256}$ initialized to
$0.10$,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:context}
\mathbf{S}_1=
\mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(
\mathbf{S}_0+
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{c}\odot
\mathrm{Up}\!\left(\rho(\mathrm{DAPPM}(\mathbf{X}))\right)
\right).
\end{equation}

\label{subsec:fusion}
The context-enhanced semantic feature is projected from 256 to 96 channels and
upsampled to $1/8$ resolution, producing $\tilde{\mathbf{S}}$. Detail and semantic
features are batch-normalized independently, concatenated, and compressed by a
$1\times1$ convolution,
%
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}=
\omega\!\left(
[
\mathrm{BN}_{d}(\mathbf{D}_2);
\mathrm{BN}_{s}(\tilde{\mathbf{S}})
]
\right).
\end{equation}
%
A second residual spatial gate $\mathbf{G}_{f}$ is computed from the detail
anchor and $\mathbf{M}$. The fused feature is
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fusion}
\mathbf{F}_{f}=
\mathcal{R}_{dw}\!\left(
\mathrm{ReLU}\!\left[
\mathbf{D}_2+
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{f}\odot
\mathbf{G}_{f}\odot\mathbf{M}
\right]\right),
\end{equation}
%
where $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{96}$ is initialized to $0.10$ and
$\mathcal{R}_{dw}$ is one RepDepthwiseBlock.

\subsection{Training objective}
\label{subsec:loss}

The current objective contains a main segmentation loss and a training-only
semantic deep-supervision loss,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loss}
\mathcal{L}=
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathrm{main}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{cl}}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{clDice}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{aux}}(t)
\left(
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathrm{aux}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{auxDice}}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Dice}}^{\mathrm{aux}}
\right).
\end{equation}
%
No separate area-Dice term is applied to the main output.

The full-resolution two-class logits are optimized with weighted
cross-entropy. In the default experiments the background weight is 1 and the
road-class weight is fixed to
%
\begin{equation}
w_{\mathrm{road}}=2.0.
\end{equation}

Topology is supervised directly on the main road probability map using soft
clDice~\cite{shit2021cldice}. Let $\mathrm{Skel}(\cdot)$ denote
differentiable morphological soft skeletonization. Topological precision and
sensitivity are
%
\begin{align}
T_{\mathrm{prec}} &=
\frac{\langle \mathrm{Skel}(\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{Y}\rangle+\epsilon}
{\|\mathrm{Skel}(\mathbf{P})\|_1+\epsilon},\\
T_{\mathrm{sens}} &=
\frac{\langle \mathrm{Skel}(\mathbf{Y}),\mathbf{P}\rangle+\epsilon}
{\|\mathrm{Skel}(\mathbf{Y})\|_1+\epsilon},
\end{align}
%
and
%
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{clDice}}=
1-
\frac{2T_{\mathrm{prec}}T_{\mathrm{sens}}}
{T_{\mathrm{prec}}+T_{\mathrm{sens}}+\epsilon}.
\end{equation}
%
The current configuration uses $\lambda_{\mathrm{cl}}=0.5$ and eight
soft-skeleton iterations. To reduce the cost on $1024\times1024$ crops, the
prediction is average-pooled and the binary target is max-pooled by a factor
of four before the clDice computation.

During training, the context-enhanced semantic feature $\mathbf{S}_1$
(Section~\ref{subsec:dappm}) is passed to a lightweight two-class semantic
head,
%
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{aux}}=
\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1}^{64\rightarrow2}
\left(
\phi\!\left(
\mathrm{BN}\!\left(
\mathrm{Conv}_{3\times3}^{256\rightarrow64}
(\mathbf{S}_1)
\right)\right)\right),
\end{equation}
%
which supplies direct road supervision to the semantic branch at $1/16$ resolution.
It is not a centerline head and is evaluated only during training. The binary
road target is adaptively max-pooled to the spatial size of
$\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{aux}}$ so thin roads survive the reduction. The auxiliary
head is trained with weighted cross-entropy plus a soft Dice term,
%
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{aux}}=
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathrm{aux}}+
0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Dice}}^{\mathrm{aux}}.
\end{equation}
%
Its maximum contribution is 0.15. The weight is zero during the first five
epochs and is linearly ramped to its maximum over the following five epochs.
This deep supervision is not executed at inference.

\subsection{Sampling and augmentation}
\label{subsec:augmentation}

Uniform random crops over scenes containing two to five percent road pixels
produce many crops with no positive pixel at all. Crops are therefore guided by
the label with probability 0.60: the mask is max-pooled by a factor of eight to a
grid of road-containing cells, one such cell is sampled uniformly, and the crop
offset is jittered so the road is not always centered. Up to eight candidates are
drawn, the candidate with the highest road fraction is kept, and the search stops
early once the fraction reaches 0.002. Images smaller than the crop are
reflect-padded, and their masks are zero-padded.

Geometric augmentation is restricted to the dihedral group of order eight,
horizontal and vertical flips combined with multiples of $90^{\circ}$ rotation,
which is label-preserving because overhead imagery has no privileged vertical
direction. Photometric augmentation applies brightness and contrast jitter
($p = 0.60$), saturation jitter ($p = 0.35$), Gaussian blur ($p = 0.15$), and
Gaussian noise ($p = 0.15$).

The current baseline does not use synthetic road-guided occlusion:
\texttt{road\_occlusion\_probability}=0.0. The training code retains this
augmentation only as an optional experiment; it is not part of the current
architecture or default results.

\subsection{Implementation details}
\label{subsec:implementation}

The encoder is a ResNet-34~\cite{he2016resnet} initialized from ImageNet
weights~\cite{deng2009imagenet}. The current default architecture uses
$C_d=96$ detail channels, a 192-channel $1/32$-resolution semantic projection, a
32-channel internal DAPPM width, pooling grids $\{1,2,4,8\}$, detail-stage
depths $(2,2)$, decoder widths 64/32/24, and dropout 0.05. All parameters are
trainable from the first epoch in the current from-ImageNet experiments;
progressive unfreezing is disabled.

Training uses $1024\times1024$ crops. For the current two-GPU configuration,
the per-device batch size is eight with one gradient-accumulation step, giving
an effective batch size of 16. The optimizer uses a base learning rate of
$1\times10^{-4}$ and weight decay $1\times10^{-4}$. The dual-branch module
uses a learning-rate factor of 0.75, ResNet layers2--4 use 0.20, and the stem
plus layer1 use 0.10. A five-epoch linear warmup from 0.10 of the target rate
is followed by cosine annealing to 2\% of the base rate. Gradients are clipped
to a norm of 3.0.

Mixed-precision training and channels-last memory format are enabled. An
exponential moving average with decay 0.999 is maintained and used for
validation/checkpoint selection. Encoder batch-normalization statistics are
kept frozen while the weights remain trainable.

Training was conducted on the Kaggle platform using two NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPUs
with 16~GB of memory per GPU. The implementation was based on PyTorch
[PYTORCH\_VERSION] with CUDA [CUDA\_VERSION]. DeepGlobe models were trained for
120 epochs. All experiments used a fixed random seed of 42 for model
initialization and data sampling, while a split seed of 3407 was used to
construct the DeepGlobe training, and test sets.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol and metrics}
\label{subsec:metrics}

Images are never resized for evaluation. A sliding window of $1024 \times 1024$
with an overlap of 256 pixels, giving a stride of 768, traverses each scene, and
tiles smaller than the window are reflect-padded. Each tile is weighted by a
separable two-dimensional Hann window clamped below at 0.05 and accumulated in
the logit domain; the accumulated logits are divided by the accumulated weights,
which removes tile seams. Writing $\mathbf{W}^{(p)}$ for the window at position
$p$ and $\mathbf{Z}^{(p)}$ for the tile logits placed in scene coordinates,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:blend}
\mathbf{Z} = \frac{\sum_p \mathbf{W}^{(p)} \odot \mathbf{Z}^{(p)}}
                  {\sum_p \mathbf{W}^{(p)}} .
\end{equation}

Road IoU is computed from a confusion matrix pooled over the whole split. Let
$\mathrm{TP}$, $\mathrm{FP}$, and $\mathrm{FN}$ count road-class true positives,
false positives, and false negatives. The primary metric is the road-class
intersection-over-union,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:iou}
\mathrm{IoU}_{\mathrm{road}} =
\frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP} + \mathrm{FP} + \mathrm{FN}},
\end{equation}
%
reported as a micro-average over pooled counts. Background IoU and mean IoU
follow from the same pooled matrix, and a macro variant of road IoU averaged
over images is reported alongside the micro value because the two diverge
when road density varies strongly across scenes.

Precision and recall follow the aggregation of the released WeavingUnet
evaluation code~\cite{weavingunet}, which this study adopts throughout for
comparability with the literature values compiled in
Section~\ref{subsec:sota}. Writing $\mathrm{TP}_n$, $\mathrm{FP}_n$, and
$\mathrm{FN}_n$ for the confusion counts of image $n$ among the $N$
evaluation images, precision and recall are averaged per image
(macro-average) rather than pooled,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prf}
\mathrm{Precision}_{\mathrm{road}} =
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}
\frac{\mathrm{TP}_n}{\mathrm{TP}_n+\mathrm{FP}_n},
\qquad
\mathrm{Recall}_{\mathrm{road}} =
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}
\frac{\mathrm{TP}_n}{\mathrm{TP}_n+\mathrm{FN}_n},
\end{equation}
%
and F1 is their harmonic mean. This aggregation is used for every precision,
recall, and F1 value reported in this paper; road IoU always uses the
pooled definition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:iou} instead, since the two aggregations
coincide for IoU.

Route quality is quantified by a relaxed F1 score with a tolerance of three
pixels, which corresponds to three meters on Massachusetts imagery and 1.5 meters
on DeepGlobe imagery. Let $\mathcal{D}_b(\cdot)$ denote binary dilation by $b$
pixels. Relaxed precision counts predicted road pixels that fall inside the
dilated label, relaxed recall counts label pixels that fall inside the dilated
prediction, and the relaxed F1 is their harmonic mean,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:relaxedf1}
\mathrm{P}_{b} =
\frac{\left|\hat{\mathbf{Y}} \cap \mathcal{D}_b(\mathbf{Y})\right|}
     {\left|\hat{\mathbf{Y}}\right|},
\qquad
\mathrm{R}_{b} =
\frac{\left|\mathbf{Y} \cap \mathcal{D}_b(\hat{\mathbf{Y}})\right|}
     {\left|\mathbf{Y}\right|} .
\end{equation}
%
The tolerance absorbs the one- to two-pixel jitter of hand-drawn road labels, so
the score reflects whether a route was found rather than whether its boundary was
placed exactly.

On the Massachusetts test split, the comparative experimental results are shown in Table~\ref{tab:sota-results} (accuracy) and Table~\ref{tab:sota-eff} (computational cost). The experimental results demonstrate that DBR-Net achieves the highest score on all four metrics, reaching 75.84\% precision, 73.45\% recall, 74.06\% F1, and 61.48\% IoU. Compared with the second best model, WeavingUnet, DBR-Net improves precision, recall, F1, and IoU by 1.92, 0.15, 1.54, and 0.47 percentage points, respectively. DBR-Net also achieves higher IoU, F1-score, and precision than other road-specific methods such as D-LinkNet, NL-LinkNet, DBRANet, SGCN, RCFSNet, MSMDFFNet, and StripUnet. Meanwhile, DBR-Net significantly outperforms general-purpose segmentation methods, including U-Net, DeepLabV3+, UPerNet with Swin-T, UPerNet with ConvNeXt, and MACU-Net, across all evaluation metrics. The recall margin over WeavingUnet is narrow (73.45\% versus 73.30\%), so the two models are effectively tied on that metric, while DBR-Net's advantage on precision, F1, and IoU is more pronounced. It also maintains a favorable computational trade-off, requiring only 22.35M parameters and 90.38G FLOPs, substantially lower than many competing road-specific methods, while also consuming considerably less memory than several high-performing architectures. These results indicate that DBR-Net provides a strong balance between segmentation accuracy and computational efficiency.

To test the adaptability and efficiency of DBRNet in varied road scenarios, we ran comparative experiments on the DeepGlobe dataset. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:sota-results}, DBRNet achieves significant improvements in Recall, F1, and IoU scored compared with other models. Especially, its IoU outperform the second-best model, WeavingUnet, by 0.75\%, and it is the only model with an Recall score exceeding 85\%. Moreover, compared with its performance on the Massachusetts dataset, DBRNet exhibits a substantially higher recall on DeepGlobe, suggesting a stronger ability to recover road pixels under more diverse road and scene conditions. Although its precision is not the highest, DBRNet still maintains a competitive precision of 81.72\%, indicating reliable road predictions with a relatively low false-positive rate.

The pattern differs between the two benchmarks. On DeepGlobe, whose
0.5\,m/pixel imagery and larger share of narrow rural and unpaved roads make
omission the dominant error mode, the advantage over WeavingUnet is
concentrated in recall rather than precision, consistent with the persistent
$1/8$-resolution detail stream and the directional strip-pooling context recovering thin road segments that a heavier,
single-resolution decoder misses. On Massachusetts, whose 1\,m/pixel imagery
covers denser suburban and forested scenes, DBR-Net instead leads mainly
through precision, and its recall margin over the closest baseline
(WeavingUnet) is much narrower than on DeepGlobe; this pattern may reflect
the one-way semantic guidance and road-aware fusion suppressing
background confusers such as driveways and building outlines more
effectively than they recover additional road pixels in these denser scenes.
The accuracy advantage is therefore uneven in magnitude across metrics on
either benchmark, but road IoU, the primary metric of this study, and every
other reported metric, is highest for DBR-Net on both datasets.

semantic stream, before Strip Pooling adds directional context
. Collapsing it to a single raw
$1/8$-resolution projection reduces IoU by 1.92 points (61.48\%$\rightarrow$59.56\%) and F1
by 1.39 points (74.06\%$\rightarrow$72.67\%) -- the largest degradation of
the three ablations by a wide margin, seven to nine times the cost of
removing either context module alone. The drop
is asymmetric across the two component metrics: precision falls 2.42
points (75.84\%$\rightarrow$73.42\%) while recall moves by only 0.43
points, so the network without this stage still locates a similar
fraction of true road pixels but labels substantially more background as
road. A raw $1/8$-resolution projection is only a linear map of the shared
encoder feature; without the two RepVGG refinement stages and the
semantic-to-detail residual, it has neither the capacity nor the
guidance to separate road-shaped clutter from actual road surface.
Because Road-Aware Fusion, the decoder, and the training objective all
operate on the representation this stage produces, its removal affects
every downstream stage rather than a single local prediction, which is
consistent with the size of the resulting drop.

With both RepVGG stages and the semantic-to-detail guidance left intact,
removing only Strip Pooling costs 0.22 points of IoU and 0.21 points of F1
(74.06\%$\rightarrow$73.85\%); precision and recall fall by a comparable
0.88 and 0.50 points. Strip Pooling therefore acts on a representation the
rest of the detail stage has already made road-discriminative, adding
horizontal and vertical context rather than supplying the discriminative
signal in the first place.

Removing Progressive DAPPM, which gathers $1/32$-resolution context and
injects it into the pretrained $1/16$-resolution anchor
, costs
0.27 points of IoU and 0.38 points of F1. Recall is unaffected
(73.45\%, identical to the full model); the entire loss comes from a
0.72-point drop in precision. DAPPM's contribution is therefore one-sided:
it helps suppress background regions that resemble roads without recovering
additional true road pixels, matching its role as a semantic-discrimination
signal rather than a detection signal.

Road-Aware Fusion is not ablated for a structural reason. Strip Pooling,
the detail stage, and DAPPM are each removed by falling back to a
well-defined, smaller version of the same path: respectively, passing
$\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{rep}}$ through unchanged, projecting $\mathbf{F}^{(8)}$
directly to the decoder, and keeping the semantic anchor $\mathbf{S}_0$
without $1/32$-resolution context. Road-Aware Fusion has no equivalent fallback:
it is the only point at which the detail and semantic representations are
combined, so removing it leaves no defined
input for the decoder at all, rather than a smaller version of the same
architecture. An ablation would therefore have to substitute an arbitrary
alternative merge rule, such as a plain sum or concatenation, which tests a
different fusion design rather than the absence of fusion, so it is
excluded from Table~\ref{tab:ablation} on those grounds.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} summarizes all three variants together. Strip
Pooling and DAPPM each cost a fraction of a point of IoU on their own, and
in opposite ways -- Strip Pooling affects precision and recall evenly,
DAPPM affects precision only -- while losing the detail stage that carries
both of them is nearly an order of magnitude more costly. The three
ablations point to the same conclusion from different angles: DBR-Net's
accuracy depends primarily on keeping a persistent, semantically guided
$1/8$-resolution stream intact, and only secondarily on the two smaller modules
that refine each branch's context.

The re-parameterized deploy form reduces compute by 0.44 GMACs and peak
allocated memory by 0.69 GiB (709 MiB) relative to the training-time
multi-branch form, consistent with folding the RepVGG and RepDepthwise
branches into single convolutions rather than changing the represented
function. Single-image latency on Tesla T4 x 2 GPU falls from 76.08\,ms to
63.98\,ms, a reduction of 12.11\,ms (15.9\%), corresponding to a throughput
increase from 13.14 to 15.63 images per second.

The re-parameterization is algebraic: batch normalization is folded into its
preceding convolution, compatible kernels are padded to a common support, and
the branch kernels and biases are summed. A RepVGGBlock becomes one dense
$3\times3$ convolution. In a RepDepthwiseBlock the $3\times3$,
$1\times5$, $5\times1$, and identity spatial branches become one depthwise
$5\times5$ convolution, followed by the existing pointwise $1\times1$
convolution. The latency reduction is consistent with this algebraic
collapse rather than with any change to the represented function.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper introduces DBR-Net, a dual branch road extraction network that efficiently captures long-range dependencies in remote sensing images. In DBR-Net, we present a Detail Stage 1, 2 and strip pooling that significantly enhances the model's local context modeling capabilities. Meanwhile, the semantic branch keeps the pretrained E4 feature as it anchor while the E5 feature is processed by the progressive DAPPM to aggregate multi-scale context. To effectively bridge these two branche, a Road-Aware Fusion is designed to preserve the detail feature and semantic feature.

DBR-Net
reaches a road IoU of 61.48\% on Massachusetts and 72.19\% on DeepGlobe, the
highest values among the compared methods. DBR-Net also maintains high computational efficiency, with only 22.35 M parameters, 90.38 G FLOPs, and 4.04 GiB memory usage, while achieving practical inference speed on Tesla T4 GPUs.

Despite these advantages, DBR-Net still has several limitations. The model has been evaluated on a limited number of road extraction datasets, and its generalization across different geographic regions, sensors, and imaging conditions requires further investigation. In addition, the current CNN-based architecture may still have limitations in modeling long-range dependencies and global road structures. In future work, we will therefore focus on evaluating DBR-Net on more diverse datasets and incorporating Transformer-based components or other global-context modeling strategies to further improve extraction accuracy and road connectivity.

\begin{abstract}
Automatic road extraction from high-resolution satellite and aerial imagery
supports map updating, logistics planning, and post-disaster response. The task
remains difficult because roads are thin, elongated structures, are frequently
occluded by trees, shadows, and vehicles, and must remain connected for
downstream routing applications. This paper presents DBR-Net, a dual-branch
segmentation network that separates high-resolution road geometry from
low-resolution semantic context. A ResNet-34 encoder is shared up to stride~8.
The detail branch remains at stride~8, where two RepVGG blocks extract local
geometry, a one-way semantic-to-detail residual injects stride-16 semantic
evidence through a zero-initialized spatial gate, and two further RepVGG blocks
refine the guided representation. A lightweight residual strip-pooling module
then aggregates long-range horizontal and vertical context without reducing the
detail resolution. In parallel, the semantic branch uses the pretrained
stride-16 and stride-32 encoder features; the stride-32 map is projected to
192 channels and processed by a progressive DAPPM whose internal branches use
32 channels and pooling grids $\{1,2,4,8\}$. The resulting context is projected
back to 256 channels and added residually to the stride-16 semantic feature.
The two streams are subsequently combined by a road-aware fusion module that
keeps the detail feature as the residual anchor and uses both a learnable
per-channel scale and a zero-initialized spatial gate before directional
depthwise refinement. A lightweight decoder reconstructs the road mask using
stride-4 and stride-2 encoder skips. Training uses weighted cross-entropy and
soft clDice on the main prediction together with a training-only stride-16
semantic deep-supervision head optimized by cross-entropy and Dice loss.
RepVGG and directional depthwise refinement blocks are structurally
re-parameterizable for efficient deployment. On the Massachusetts Roads and
DeepGlobe benchmarks, the proposed architecture is evaluated with
native-resolution sliding-window inference; final quantitative results are
reported in Section~\ref{sec:results-discussion}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Road networks are a primary layer of nearly every geospatial information system,
and keeping that layer current is a recurring cost. New construction, closures,
and damage after floods or earthquakes change the network faster than manual
digitization can follow. High-resolution optical satellite and aerial imagery is
now acquired at sub-meter to meter ground sampling distance over most inhabited
areas, which makes automated extraction of road surfaces from such imagery a
practical route to map maintenance \cite{luweng2025roadreview}. The applications
that consume the result, including routing, logistics, and the allocation of
emergency access after a disaster, depend less on the exact area of the predicted
road surface than on whether the extracted network remains connected.

Road extraction is usually posed as binary semantic segmentation, yet several
properties separate it from the object-centric problems that drive most
architectural progress. Roads occupy roughly two to five percent of the pixels in
a typical scene, so an unweighted pixel objective converges toward a near-empty
prediction and pixel accuracy carries almost no information. At one meter per
pixel a two-lane road is between eight and twenty pixels wide; a feature map at
stride 32 represents it with less than one pixel, and no decoder can reliably
reconstruct what the encoder has already discarded. The structures are also
anisotropic and long-range: rivers, field boundaries, fences, and elongated
rooftops are locally indistinguishable from roads, and only regional context
resolves the ambiguity. Occlusion compounds the difficulty, because a five-pixel
gap caused by a tree canopy leaves the intersection-over-union nearly unchanged
while destroying an entire route. Evaluation protocols that report only pixel
overlap therefore reward the wrong behavior.

Two lines of work address these properties. The first adapts general-purpose
encoder-decoder segmentation networks to aerial imagery, from U-Net
\cite{ronneberger2015unet} and Deep Residual U-Net \cite{zhang2018resunet} to
D-LinkNet \cite{zhou2018dlinknet}, and then improves feature aggregation through
multi-resolution exchange \cite{wang2021hrnet} or attention decoders
\cite{xie2021segformer}. The second supervises connectivity directly, either
through auxiliary geometric targets such as road orientation
\cite{batra2019connectivity} and connectivity attention \cite{mei2021coanet}, or
through topology-aware objectives such as clDice \cite{shit2021cldice} and the
asymmetric Tversky loss \cite{salehi2017tversky}.
Section~\ref{sec:related-work} reviews both lines in detail.

Three issues remain unresolved across them. Preserving a high-resolution stream
is effective, yet the usual mechanism, symmetric exchange among all resolution
pairs \cite{wang2021hrnet, yu2021bisenetv2, pan2023ddrnet}, spends part of its
budget on exchanges a thin-structure task cannot use: a stride-32 map cannot
represent an eight-pixel road, so requiring it to participate in a
detail-preserving exchange contributes little. Connectivity supervision is
likewise effective, but published formulations either depend on external
morphological libraries, which complicates reproducibility and distributed
training, or skeletonize after the label has been downsampled, which merges
narrow branches and intersections before they can be supervised. The third issue
is discussed less often: multi-branch blocks and parallel context modules that
improve accuracy also slow inference on the large scenes operational mapping
requires, and structural re-parameterization \cite{ding2021repvgg}, which removes
that cost exactly, has to our knowledge not been combined with a road-specific
detail-preserving design. An architecture is needed in which the detail stream is
preserved by construction, the semantic stream contributes only where it helps,
connectivity is supervised without external dependencies, and the multi-branch
machinery collapses into a single-path network for deployment.

DBR-Net is designed around those requirements. The network shares a
ResNet-34 encoder up to stride~8 and then assigns distinct responsibilities to
two branches. The detail branch remains at stride~8 throughout the
architecture. It first refines the shared feature with two RepVGG blocks,
receives a single \emph{semantic-to-detail} residual from the pretrained
stride-16 feature, and is refined by two further RepVGG blocks followed by
strip pooling. There is deliberately no detail-to-semantic route. The semantic
branch remains anchored by the pretrained stride-16 feature, while its
stride-32 feature is used only to collect broad context through a progressive
DAPPM. The context is projected back to 256 channels and added residually to
the stride-16 semantic representation.

The complete detail and semantic representations are combined only in a
dedicated road-aware fusion module. The two inputs are normalized
independently, concatenated and compressed to the detail width. A
zero-initialized spatial gate determines where the mixed semantic evidence
should act, while a learnable per-channel scale determines its magnitude. The
detail stream remains the residual anchor, which prevents the fusion stage from
overwriting the high-resolution road geometry. A re-parameterizable
directional depthwise block then refines the fused feature.

Connectivity is encouraged directly on the main prediction using soft clDice
\cite{shit2021cldice}, rather than through the former centerline-Tversky
auxiliary head. A separate training-only semantic head provides deep
supervision at stride~16 using a weighted cross-entropy and Dice objective.
This auxiliary head is intended to make the semantic branch explicitly
road-discriminative; it is not a centerline predictor and is not executed
during inference.

The experimental program evaluates the model on the Massachusetts Roads
dataset \cite{mnih2013aerial} and the DeepGlobe road extraction dataset
\cite{demir2018deepglobe}. Evaluation is performed at native resolution with
overlapping tiles blended in the logit domain. The primary metric is road-class
intersection-over-union, with precision, recall, F1 and a relaxed F1 score
reported as complementary measures.

The contributions of the current architecture are summarized as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
\item A detail-preserving dual-branch architecture in which the road-detail
      representation is maintained at stride~8 while the semantic branch uses
      stride~16/32 features only for semantic discrimination and broad context.
\item A one-way semantic-to-detail residual with a zero-initialized spatial
      gate and learnable per-channel scale. The reverse detail-to-semantic route
      is intentionally removed, so noisy high-resolution texture does not
      perturb the semantic stream.
\item A lightweight road-detail refinement pathway that combines
      re-parameterizable RepVGG blocks with residual horizontal/vertical strip
      pooling, followed by a road-aware final fusion that keeps detail as the
      residual anchor.
\item A topology-aware training objective consisting of weighted
      cross-entropy and soft clDice on the main output, together with a
      training-only stride-16 semantic deep-supervision head using
      cross-entropy and Dice loss.
\item A deployment-oriented implementation in which the RepVGG and directional
      depthwise refinement blocks can be structurally re-parameterized into
      single convolutional paths without changing the represented function
      beyond floating-point round-off.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Encoder-decoder networks for road extraction}
\label{subsec:rw-encdec}

Road extraction moved to fully convolutional models with the release of the
Massachusetts Roads dataset \cite{mnih2013aerial}, which established patch-based
learning of road masks from aerial imagery as a benchmark task. U-Net
\cite{ronneberger2015unet} introduced the skip-connected encoder-decoder that
most later road models inherit: the encoder builds semantic capacity by
downsampling, and lateral skips return spatial detail to the decoder. Deep
Residual U-Net \cite{zhang2018resunet} replaced the plain encoder with residual
units and reported improved road delineation on Massachusetts with fewer
parameters than the original.

The DeepGlobe challenge \cite{demir2018deepglobe} shifted attention to
0.5\,m/pixel satellite imagery and larger scene variety. Its winning entry,
D-LinkNet \cite{zhou2018dlinknet}, inserted a cascade of dilated convolutions at
the encoder bottleneck, enlarging the receptive field without an additional
downsampling stage, and it remains the reference baseline on that benchmark. The
shared limitation of this family is structural rather than incidental. Skip
connections return detail that survived the encoder, but a road eight to twenty
pixels wide is represented by a fraction of a pixel at stride 32, so what the
decoder receives through the deepest skips is context, not geometry. Dilation
mitigates the problem by avoiding one downsampling step; it does not remove the
dependence on a deeply downsampled trunk.

\subsection{Multi-resolution and dual-branch architectures}
\label{subsec:rw-multires}

A second design maintains high resolution throughout the network instead of
recovering it. HRNet \cite{wang2021hrnet} runs parallel branches at several
resolutions and repeatedly exchanges information among all of them, which keeps a
high-resolution representation available at every depth. The approach is
effective for position-sensitive tasks, but the exchange is dense: every pair of
resolutions is fused at every stage, and the cost grows with the number of
branches.

Real-time segmentation networks reduce that cost to two branches. BiSeNet~V2
\cite{yu2021bisenetv2} pairs a shallow detail branch with a deeper semantic
branch and merges them once through a guided aggregation layer. DDRNet
\cite{pan2023ddrnet} keeps two resolutions throughout and exchanges between them
repeatedly, and introduces the deep aggregation pyramid pooling module to enlarge
the receptive field at the low-resolution branch. Both were designed for
street-level traffic scenes, where objects are large and the detail branch mainly
sharpens boundaries. Overhead road imagery inverts that balance: the structures
of interest are themselves near the resolution limit, so which resolutions
exchange, and under what control, matters more than how many exchanges occur.
Neither design gates the exchange spatially, and neither is initialized so that
adding the exchange leaves a pretrained encoder undisturbed.

Context aggregation modules developed for scene parsing are used across both
families. Pyramid pooling \cite{zhao2017pspnet} and atrous spatial pyramid
pooling \cite{chen2018deeplabv3plus} both compute several receptive-field scales
in parallel and combine them once at the end. The deep aggregation variant
\cite{pan2023ddrnet} adds hierarchical residual connections among the pooling
branches. These modules are normally built on batch normalization, which becomes
unreliable at the small per-device batch sizes that 1024-pixel remote sensing
crops impose, particularly for a branch that pools to a single spatial location.

\subsection{Connectivity and topology-aware supervision}
\label{subsec:rw-topology}

Because pixel overlap is insensitive to short breaks, a separate line of work
supervises connectivity explicitly. Batra et al.~\cite{batra2019connectivity}
predict a road orientation field jointly with the mask and use it in a stacked
refinement stage that reconnects fragmented predictions. CoANet
\cite{mei2021coanet} matches the network to road geometry directly, using strip
convolutions along four directions together with a connectivity attention module
that predicts whether neighboring locations belong to the same road.

Loss functions attack the same problem without changing the architecture. Region
losses of the Dice family \cite{milletari2016vnet} and reweighted classification
losses \cite{lin2017focal} counteract the imbalance itself, and the Tversky loss
\cite{salehi2017tversky} generalizes Dice with separate weights for false
positives and false negatives, so an application can declare which error is worse.
clDice \cite{shit2021cldice} measures overlap between the skeletons of prediction
and label, which makes a break expensive even when it costs few pixels, and
provides a differentiable soft skeletonization built from morphological
operations.

Two practical constraints in this literature motivate the present design. First,
skeleton-based supervision is often implemented with external morphological
libraries operating on CPU arrays, which complicates distributed training and
makes the target dependent on a library version. Second, when the auxiliary head
sits at a reduced stride, the natural implementation downsamples the label and
then thins it; that order merges narrow branches and intersections, which are
precisely the structures the term is meant to protect.

\subsection{Structural re-parameterization}
\label{subsec:rw-reparam}

Multi-branch blocks improve optimization but cost inference time. RepVGG
\cite{ding2021repvgg} showed that a training-time block of parallel $3\times3$,
$1\times1$, and identity paths, each followed by batch normalization, can be
folded algebraically into a single $3\times3$ convolution, so the deployed model
is a plain sequential stack with no accuracy loss. The transformation is exact:
batch normalization folds into the preceding convolution, and kernels padded to a
common size sum linearly.

Re-parameterization has been applied mainly to classification backbones and
general-purpose detectors. In the present network, the detail stages use the
standard ungrouped RepVGG formulation, whereas the post-fusion and decoder
refinement stages use a depthwise variant with $3\times3$, $1\times5$, and
$5\times1$ spatial branches followed by pointwise channel mixing. The
anisotropic branches are motivated by the elongated geometry of road segments
and fuse into a single $5\times5$ depthwise kernel at deployment.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Problem formulation and notation}
\label{subsec:formulation}

Let $\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times H \times W}$ denote an optical RGB image
of height $H$ and width $W$, and let
$\mathbf{Y} \in \{0,1\}^{H \times W}$ denote the corresponding label, where
$Y_{ij} = 1$ marks a road-surface pixel. The task is to learn a mapping
$f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ that produces two-channel logits at the input
resolution,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:task}
\mathbf{Z} = f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{I}) \in
\mathbb{R}^{2 \times H \times W},
\end{equation}
%
where $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ collects all trainable parameters. The road
probability map $\mathbf{P} \in [0,1]^{H \times W}$ and the predicted mask
$\hat{\mathbf{Y}}$ follow from the channel-wise softmax and a decision threshold
$\tau$,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:threshold}
P_{ij} = \frac{\exp(Z_{1ij})}{\exp(Z_{0ij}) + \exp(Z_{1ij})},
\qquad
\hat{Y}_{ij} = \mathbb{1}\!\left[P_{ij} \ge \tau\right],
\end{equation}
%
with $\mathbb{1}[\cdot]$ the indicator function. The default threshold is
$\tau = 0.5$; Section~\ref{subsec:metrics} describes how a calibrated threshold
is estimated on validation data only.

Feature tensors are written $\mathbf{F}^{(s)}
\in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H/s \times W/s}$, where the superscript is the output
stride $s$ relative to the input. Scalars are italic lowercase, vectors bold
lowercase, and tensors bold uppercase. Throughout the paper, $\mathbf{D}$ denotes
the detail stream and $\mathbf{S}$ the semantic stream.

In this section, we introduce the overall network and the details of the modules. Fig 1 describes the overall structure of the proposed model including an encoder, Detail stage, DAPPM, Road-Aware Fusion and the decoder. Especially, remote sensing image is handled by the encoder which is based on a Resnet34 network. In general, it can be seen that there are four stages, containing 3, 4, 6, 3 blocks, and each block has convolutional layers (Conv), batch normalization (BN), and ReLU activation functions. This encoding process create multiscale feature maps with five different resolutions such as E1, E2, E3, E4, and E5. The feature map E3 is connected Detail Stage 1 to provide a high-resolution spatial representation for preserving exactly road geometry. After receiving information from E4, the feature is further refined by Detail Stage 2 and passed throug the Strip Pooling module to take long-range directional context while remaining fine road geometry. Within the semantic branc, E4 serves as the semantic anchor, while the deepest feature map E5 is processed by the ProgressiveDAPPM to aggregate multi-scale long-range contextual information. The resulting context is projected, upsampled, and residually fused with E4 to strengthen the semantic representation before Road-Aware Fusion.  

Next, the feature map is upsampled to the stride-4 resolution and fused with a skip connection from E2. The shallow E2 feature is first projected to a lower-dimensional space and then concatenated with the decoder feature to recover fine spatial details. The combined representation is compressed and refined before being further upsampled to stride 2, where a second skip connection from E1 is introduced. This progressive reconstruction process enables the decoder to recover road boundaries and local geometric structures while gradually restoring the original spatial resolution.

\subsection{Re-parameterizable building blocks}
\label{subsec:reparam}

The refinement stages of every module are built from two block types that are
multi-branch during training and single-branch at inference, following the
re-parameterization principle of RepVGG~\cite{ding2021repvgg}. Introducing them
first makes the module descriptions that follow self-contained.

\paragraph{RepVGGBlock.} A standard-convolution block combining a $3 \times 3$
convolution, a $1 \times 1$ convolution, and an identity path, each followed by
batch normalization. It is used for the detail stream, where full channel mixing
is required.

\paragraph{RepDepthwiseBlock.} A depthwise block combining $3 \times 3$,
$1 \times 5$, and $5 \times 1$ depthwise convolutions with an identity path,
followed by a pointwise convolution. The two anisotropic kernels are chosen for
the elongated geometry of road segments, so the block can learn horizontal and
vertical responses separately rather than approximating both with one square
kernel. It is used wherever refinement follows a fusion step, in the decoder and
at the output of the controlled fusion module.

Fusion into a single convolution proceeds in two stages. First, a convolution
with weight $\mathbf{W}$ and bias $\mathbf{b}$ followed by batch normalization
with running mean $\mu_c$, running variance $v_c$, scale $\beta_c$, shift
$\delta_c$, and stability constant $\epsilon$ is equivalent to a single
convolution whose parameters, for output channel $c$, are
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bnfold}
\mathbf{W}'_c = \frac{\beta_c}{\sqrt{v_c + \epsilon}}\,\mathbf{W}_c,
\qquad
b'_c = \delta_c +
\frac{\beta_c\,(b_c - \mu_c)}{\sqrt{v_c + \epsilon}} .
\end{equation}
%
Second, every branch is folded by Eq.~\eqref{eq:bnfold}, the resulting kernels
are zero-padded to the largest kernel size in the block, and the padded kernels
and biases are summed,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:repsum}
\mathbf{W}^{\star} = \sum_{m} \mathrm{Pad}\!\left(\mathbf{W}'_{m}\right),
\qquad
\mathbf{b}^{\star} = \sum_{m} \mathbf{b}'_{m},
\end{equation}
%
where $m$ indexes the branches, including the identity path expressed as a
centered Dirac kernel. A RepVGGBlock collapses to one $3 \times 3$ convolution
with bias and a RepDepthwiseBlock to one $5 \times 5$ depthwise convolution with
bias. The transformation is algebraic and introduces no approximation beyond
floating-point rounding, which Section~\ref{subsec:efficiency} quantifies.

\subsection{Shared encoder}
\label{subsec:encoder}

The encoder is a ResNet-34~\cite{he2016resnet} truncated after its last residual
stage and initialized from ImageNet weights~\cite{deng2009imagenet}. It returns
five feature levels rather than one: a stride-2 stem with 64 channels, a stride-4
map with 64 channels, a stride-8 map with 128 channels, a stride-16 map with 256
channels, and a stride-32 map with 512 channels. The stride-8 map is the input of
the detail stream, the stride-16 and stride-32 maps feed the semantic stream, and
the stride-2 and stride-4 maps are reserved as decoder skips.

Both branches therefore start from pretrained features. This matters for the
initialization strategy used throughout the network: several modules below begin
as identity or near-identity operators specifically so that the pretrained
statistics are not disturbed before the randomly initialized parts have learned
anything useful.

\subsection{Dual-branch detail and semantic pathways}
\label{subsec:dualres}

\paragraph{Detail projection and first stage.}
The shared stride-8 encoder feature
$\mathbf{F}^{(8)}\in\mathbb{R}^{128\times H/8\times W/8}$ is first projected
to $C_d=96$ channels by a $1\times1$ convolution,
%
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{D}_0 =
\phi\!\left(\mathrm{BN}\!\left(
\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1}^{128\rightarrow96}
(\mathbf{F}^{(8)})\right)\right),
\end{equation}
%
where $\phi$ denotes ReLU. Two RepVGG blocks then produce
$\mathbf{D}_1\in\mathbb{R}^{96\times H/8\times W/8}$. No operation in the
detail branch changes its spatial resolution.

\paragraph{One-way semantic-to-detail guidance.}
The pretrained stride-16 feature
$\mathbf{S}_0\in\mathbb{R}^{256\times H/16\times W/16}$ is projected to the
detail width and bilinearly upsampled,
%
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{s\rightarrow d}
=
\mathrm{Up}_{16\rightarrow8}
\left(
\phi_{s\rightarrow d}(\mathbf{S}_0)
\right),
\end{equation}
%
where $\phi_{s\rightarrow d}$ is a $1\times1$ convolution followed by batch
normalization without an activation. The residual is modulated by a spatial
gate $\mathbf{G}_{s\rightarrow d}$ and by a learnable per-channel scale
$\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s\rightarrow d}\in\mathbb{R}^{96}$,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exchange-detail}
\mathbf{D}_{g}=
\mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(
\mathbf{D}_1+
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s\rightarrow d}
\odot
\mathbf{G}_{s\rightarrow d}
\odot
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{s\rightarrow d}
\right).
\end{equation}
%
The scale is initialized to $0.10$. Importantly, the current architecture has
\emph{no} detail-to-semantic residual.

\paragraph{Second detail stage and strip pooling.}
After semantic guidance, two further RepVGG blocks refine
$\mathbf{D}_{g}$. The resulting feature is passed through a lightweight
residual strip-pooling block inspired by the strip pooling strategy of
Hou et al.~\cite{hou2020strip}. For a reduced feature
$\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{C_r\times h\times w}$ with
$C_r=\max(16,C_d/4)=24$, horizontal and vertical descriptors are
%
\begin{align}
\mathbf{H} &=
\mathrm{Conv}_{3\times1}
\left(
\mathrm{AvgPool}_{(h,1)}(\mathbf{R})
\right),\\
\mathbf{V} &=
\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times3}
\left(
\mathrm{AvgPool}_{(1,w)}(\mathbf{R})
\right).
\end{align}
%
Both descriptors are group-normalized, activated, broadcast back to
$h\times w$, summed, and projected from 24 to 96 channels. The final detail
feature is
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:strippool}
\mathbf{D}_2 =
\mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{rep}}+
\psi\!\left(
\mathrm{ReLU}(\mathrm{Up}(\mathbf{H})+\mathrm{Up}(\mathbf{V}))
\right)
\right),
\end{equation}
%
where $\psi$ is a $1\times1$ projection. This module supplies directional
long-range context while preserving the stride-8 geometry.

\subsection{Residual spatial gate}
\label{subsec:gate}

The same lightweight spatial-gating mechanism is used at two locations: the
S16$\rightarrow$S8 semantic guidance and the final Road-Aware Fusion. For a
target feature $\mathbf{T}$ and an aligned projected source
$\boldsymbol{\Delta}$, the gate is
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gate}
\mathbf{G}=2\,\sigma\!\left(
g\!\left[
\mathrm{GN}(\mathbf{T});
\mathrm{GN}(\boldsymbol{\Delta})
\right]\right),
\end{equation}
%
where $g$ consists of a $3\times3$ convolution, group normalization, SiLU,
and a final $1\times1$ convolution to one channel. The final convolution is
initialized to zero, so $\mathbf{G}=2\sigma(0)=1$ at initialization.

\subsection{Progressive deep aggregation pyramid pooling}
\label{subsec:dappm}

The semantic context path starts from the pretrained stride-32 encoder feature
$\mathbf{F}^{(32)}\in\mathbb{R}^{512\times H/32\times W/32}$. A $1\times1$
projection reduces it to 192 channels before ProgressiveDAPPM. The module uses
an internal branch width of 32 channels and adaptive pooling grids
$\{1,2,4,8\}$; the implementation traverses them as $(8,4,2,1)$. Let
$\mathbf{X}$ denote the 192-channel projected feature and $\mathbf{U}_0$ its
32-channel $1\times1$ projection. The progressive update is
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dappm}
\mathbf{U}_{k}=
\pi_k\!\left(
\mathbf{U}_{k^{-}}+
\mathrm{Up}\!\left[
\psi_k\!\left(
\mathrm{AvgPool}_{k\times k}(\mathbf{X})
\right)\right]
\right),
\end{equation}
%
where $\psi_k$ is a $1\times1$ projection to 32 channels and $\pi_k$ is a
$3\times3$ convolution. Group normalization is used throughout the DAPPM
branches. The base projection and all progressive outputs are concatenated and
compressed back to 192 channels, then added to a 192-channel shortcut.

The DAPPM output is projected from 192 to 256 channels and bilinearly
upsampled to stride~16. It is injected into the pretrained semantic anchor with
a learnable scale $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{256}$ initialized to
$0.10$,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:context}
\mathbf{S}_1=
\mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(
\mathbf{S}_0+
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{c}\odot
\mathrm{Up}\!\left(\rho(\mathrm{DAPPM}(\mathbf{X}))\right)
\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Semantic deep supervision}
\label{subsec:semantic-aux}

During training, $\mathbf{S}_1$ is passed to a lightweight two-class semantic
head,
%
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{aux}}=
\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1}^{64\rightarrow2}
\left(
\phi\!\left(
\mathrm{BN}\!\left(
\mathrm{Conv}_{3\times3}^{256\rightarrow64}
(\mathbf{S}_1)
\right)\right)\right).
\end{equation}
%
This head supplies direct road supervision to the semantic branch at
stride~16. It is not a centerline head and is evaluated only during training.

\subsection{Road-aware fusion}
\label{subsec:fusion}

The context-enhanced semantic feature is projected from 256 to 96 channels and
upsampled to stride~8, producing $\tilde{\mathbf{S}}$. Detail and semantic
features are batch-normalized independently, concatenated, and compressed by a
$1\times1$ convolution,
%
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}=
\omega\!\left(
[
\mathrm{BN}_{d}(\mathbf{D}_2);
\mathrm{BN}_{s}(\tilde{\mathbf{S}})
]
\right).
\end{equation}
%
A second residual spatial gate $\mathbf{G}_{f}$ is computed from the detail
anchor and $\mathbf{M}$. The fused feature is
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fusion}
\mathbf{F}_{f}=
\mathcal{R}_{dw}\!\left(
\mathrm{ReLU}\!\left[
\mathbf{D}_2+
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{f}\odot
\mathbf{G}_{f}\odot\mathbf{M}
\right]\right),
\end{equation}
%
where $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{96}$ is initialized to $0.10$ and
$\mathcal{R}_{dw}$ is one RepDepthwiseBlock.

\subsection{Road reconstruction decoder}
\label{subsec:decoder}

The decoder reconstructs the road mask in three resolution steps. First,
$\mathbf{F}_{f}$ is projected from 96 to 64 channels and upsampled from
stride~8 to stride~4. The stride-4 ResNet layer1 feature is projected from 64
to 32 channels, concatenated with the decoder feature, and fused back to 64
channels with a $1\times1$ convolution. Two RepDepthwiseBlocks refine this
stride-4 representation.

The stride-4 decoder feature is then upsampled to stride~2. The stem feature is
projected from 64 to 16 channels, concatenated with the decoder feature, and
fused to 32 channels. Two more RepDepthwiseBlocks refine the stride-2 feature.
Finally, the representation is bilinearly resized to the original input
resolution, processed by a separable convolution from 32 to 24 channels,
followed by dropout with rate 0.05 and a $1\times1$ classifier producing the
two road/background logits.

There is no centerline or topology-prediction branch in the current decoder.
At inference the network returns only the full-resolution two-class road
logits.

\subsection{Training objective}
\label{subsec:loss}

The current objective contains a main segmentation loss and a training-only
semantic deep-supervision loss,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loss}
\mathcal{L}=
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathrm{main}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{cl}}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{clDice}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{aux}}(t)
\left(
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathrm{aux}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{auxDice}}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Dice}}^{\mathrm{aux}}
\right).
\end{equation}
%
No separate area-Dice term is applied to the main output.

\paragraph{Weighted main cross-entropy.}
The full-resolution two-class logits are optimized with weighted
cross-entropy. In the default experiments the background weight is 1 and the
road-class weight is fixed to
%
\begin{equation}
w_{\mathrm{road}}=2.0.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Soft clDice.}
Topology is supervised directly on the main road probability map using soft
clDice~\cite{shit2021cldice}. Let $\mathrm{Skel}(\cdot)$ denote
differentiable morphological soft skeletonization. Topological precision and
sensitivity are
%
\begin{align}
T_{\mathrm{prec}} &=
\frac{\langle \mathrm{Skel}(\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{Y}\rangle+\epsilon}
{\|\mathrm{Skel}(\mathbf{P})\|_1+\epsilon},\\
T_{\mathrm{sens}} &=
\frac{\langle \mathrm{Skel}(\mathbf{Y}),\mathbf{P}\rangle+\epsilon}
{\|\mathrm{Skel}(\mathbf{Y})\|_1+\epsilon},
\end{align}
%
and
%
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{clDice}}=
1-
\frac{2T_{\mathrm{prec}}T_{\mathrm{sens}}}
{T_{\mathrm{prec}}+T_{\mathrm{sens}}+\epsilon}.
\end{equation}
%
The current configuration uses $\lambda_{\mathrm{cl}}=0.5$ and eight
soft-skeleton iterations. To reduce the cost on $1024\times1024$ crops, the
prediction is average-pooled and the binary target is max-pooled by a factor
of four before the clDice computation.

\paragraph{Stride-16 semantic auxiliary loss.}
The binary road target is adaptively max-pooled to the spatial size of
$\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{aux}}$ so thin roads survive the reduction. The auxiliary
head is trained with weighted cross-entropy plus a soft Dice term,
%
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{aux}}=
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathrm{aux}}+
0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Dice}}^{\mathrm{aux}}.
\end{equation}
%
Its maximum contribution is 0.15. The weight is zero during the first five
epochs and is linearly ramped to its maximum over the following five epochs.
This deep supervision is not executed at inference.
\endgroup

The Massachusetts Roads dataset \cite{mnih2013aerial} provides aerial imagery of
the state of Massachusetts at one meter per pixel with official training and test
lists. Splits follow the order given in those lists rather than a random shuffle.
The first 61 entries of the test list are held out as the validation split and
the remaining 117 images form the test split, so that no image used for model
selection or threshold calibration appears in the reported test results. The
training pipeline verifies that the training and test key sets are disjoint and
aborts on any overlap.

The DeepGlobe road extraction dataset \cite{demir2018deepglobe} provides 6226
publicly labeled image and mask pairs at 0.5 meters per pixel. The distributed
validation directory ships without masks in many mirrors, so a deterministic
holdout is carved out of the labeled pool instead: the pairs are shuffled with a
fixed seed of 3407, the first 5000 form the training split, and the remaining
1226 form the test split. The first 300 images of the test split serve as the
validation split. This validation split is therefore a subset of the test split,
a protocol adopted for comparability with prior work on this benchmark. The
overlap is recorded explicitly rather than left implicit, and results on the
1226-image test split should be read with the knowledge that 300 of those images
were also used for model selection. [VERIFY: confirm that the comparison methods
in Table~\ref{tab:sota} use the same DeepGlobe protocol before claiming a
like-for-like comparison.]

Every run writes a split manifest recording the split seed, the per-split counts,
the pairwise overlap counts between splits, and the absolute path of every image
and mask pair. Native-resolution evaluation reads the same manifest, so the test
set is identical to the one fixed at training time and is never regenerated from
random indices. Labels encoded in $\{0,1\}$ and in $\{0,255\}$ are both accepted;
the binarization threshold is chosen from the maximum value of each mask, and
three-channel label images are reduced by a per-pixel channel maximum.

\subsection{Sampling and augmentation}
\label{subsec:augmentation}

Uniform random crops over scenes containing two to five percent road pixels
produce many crops with no positive pixel at all. Crops are therefore guided by
the label with probability 0.60: the mask is max-pooled by a factor of eight to a
grid of road-containing cells, one such cell is sampled uniformly, and the crop
offset is jittered so the road is not always centered. Up to eight candidates are
drawn, the candidate with the highest road fraction is kept, and the search stops
early once the fraction reaches 0.002. Images smaller than the crop are
reflect-padded, and their masks are zero-padded.

Geometric augmentation is restricted to the dihedral group of order eight,
horizontal and vertical flips combined with multiples of $90^{\circ}$ rotation,
which is label-preserving because overhead imagery has no privileged vertical
direction. Photometric augmentation applies brightness and contrast jitter
($p = 0.60$), saturation jitter ($p = 0.35$), Gaussian blur ($p = 0.15$), and
Gaussian noise ($p = 0.15$).

The current baseline does not use synthetic road-guided occlusion:
\texttt{road\_occlusion\_probability}=0.0. The training code retains this
augmentation only as an optional experiment; it is not part of the current
architecture or default results.

\subsection{Implementation details}
\label{subsec:implementation}

The encoder is a ResNet-34~\cite{he2016resnet} initialized from ImageNet
weights~\cite{deng2009imagenet}. The current default architecture uses
$C_d=96$ detail channels, a 192-channel stride-32 semantic projection, a
32-channel internal DAPPM width, pooling grids $\{1,2,4,8\}$, detail-stage
depths $(2,2)$, decoder widths 64/32/24, and dropout 0.05. All parameters are
trainable from the first epoch in the current from-ImageNet experiments;
progressive unfreezing is disabled.

Training uses $1024\times1024$ crops. For the current two-GPU configuration,
the per-device batch size is eight with one gradient-accumulation step, giving
an effective batch size of 16. The optimizer uses a base learning rate of
$1\times10^{-4}$ and weight decay $1\times10^{-4}$. The dual-branch module
uses a learning-rate factor of 0.75, ResNet layers2--4 use 0.20, and the stem
plus layer1 use 0.10. A five-epoch linear warmup from 0.10 of the target rate
is followed by cosine annealing to 2\% of the base rate. Gradients are clipped
to a norm of 3.0.

Mixed-precision training and channels-last memory format are enabled. An
exponential moving average with decay 0.999 is maintained and used for
validation/checkpoint selection. Encoder batch-normalization statistics are
kept frozen while the weights remain trainable.

Training was conducted on the Kaggle platform using two NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPUs
with 16~GB of memory per GPU. The implementation was based on PyTorch
[PYTORCH\_VERSION] with CUDA [CUDA\_VERSION]. DeepGlobe models were trained for
120 epochs. All experiments used a fixed random seed of 42 for model
initialization and data sampling, while a split seed of 3407 was used to
construct the DeepGlobe training, and test sets.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol and metrics}
\label{subsec:metrics}

Images are never resized for evaluation. A sliding window of $1024 \times 1024$
with an overlap of 256 pixels, giving a stride of 768, traverses each scene, and
tiles smaller than the window are reflect-padded. Each tile is weighted by a
separable two-dimensional Hann window clamped below at 0.05 and accumulated in
the logit domain; the accumulated logits are divided by the accumulated weights,
which removes tile seams. Writing $\mathbf{W}^{(p)}$ for the window at position
$p$ and $\mathbf{Z}^{(p)}$ for the tile logits placed in scene coordinates,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:blend}
\mathbf{Z} = \frac{\sum_p \mathbf{W}^{(p)} \odot \mathbf{Z}^{(p)}}
                  {\sum_p \mathbf{W}^{(p)}} .
\end{equation}

Metrics are computed from a confusion matrix pooled over the whole split. Let
$\mathrm{TP}$, $\mathrm{FP}$, and $\mathrm{FN}$ count road-class true positives,
false positives, and false negatives. The primary metric is the road-class
intersection-over-union,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:iou}
\mathrm{IoU}_{\mathrm{road}} =
\frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP} + \mathrm{FP} + \mathrm{FN}},
\end{equation}
%
reported as a micro-average over pooled counts. Background IoU, mean IoU,
precision, recall, and F1 follow from the same pooled matrix, and a macro variant
of road IoU averaged over images is reported alongside the micro value because
the two diverge when road density varies strongly across scenes.

Route quality is quantified by a relaxed F1 score with a tolerance of three
pixels, which corresponds to three meters on Massachusetts imagery and 1.5 meters
on DeepGlobe imagery. Let $\mathcal{D}_b(\cdot)$ denote binary dilation by $b$
pixels. Relaxed precision counts predicted road pixels that fall inside the
dilated label, relaxed recall counts label pixels that fall inside the dilated
prediction, and the relaxed F1 is their harmonic mean,
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:relaxedf1}
\mathrm{P}_{b} =
\frac{\left|\hat{\mathbf{Y}} \cap \mathcal{D}_b(\mathbf{Y})\right|}
     {\left|\hat{\mathbf{Y}}\right|},
\qquad
\mathrm{R}_{b} =
\frac{\left|\mathbf{Y} \cap \mathcal{D}_b(\hat{\mathbf{Y}})\right|}
     {\left|\mathbf{Y}\right|} .
\end{equation}
%
The tolerance absorbs the one- to two-pixel jitter of hand-drawn road labels, so
the score reflects whether a route was found rather than whether its boundary was
placed exactly.

Two thresholds are reported for every metric. The fixed setting uses
$\tau = 0.5$. The calibrated setting sweeps $\tau$ from 0.20 to 0.80 in steps of
0.02 and selects the value maximizing road IoU, using a 1001-bin histogram of
predicted probabilities per true class so that full probability maps never have
to be stored. The sweep is permitted on validation splits only; the selected
threshold is then applied unchanged to the test split.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

Several limitations bound the scope of these results. The model predicts raster
masks and does not extract a road graph, so vectorization, node and edge
recovery, and downstream routing are outside the evaluated pipeline. Roads are
treated as a single class, with no distinction between highway, urban, and
unpaved surfaces. The input is restricted to three-band optical imagery, so
multispectral, synthetic aperture radar, and time-series inputs are untested. On
DeepGlobe, the validation split is a subset of the test split, as stated in
Section~\ref{subsec:datasets}, so DeepGlobe test scores carry a model-selection
dependency that Massachusetts scores do not. Both benchmarks cover a limited
range of geographies and acquisition conditions, and generalization to regions or
sensors not represented in them is not established by these experiments.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper addresses road extraction from high-resolution optical imagery with
an architecture that separates fine road geometry from broad semantic context.
The current DBR-Net keeps a 96-channel detail representation at stride~8,
refines it with RepVGG blocks, injects only one-way stride-16 semantic guidance,
and augments it with residual horizontal/vertical Strip Pooling. The semantic
path remains independent of the detail stream, gathers stride-32 context with a
192-channel ProgressiveDAPPM representation, and folds that context back into
the 256-channel stride-16 semantic anchor.

The two representations are combined by Road-Aware Fusion, which preserves the
detail feature as the residual anchor while admitting semantic evidence through
a zero-initialized spatial gate and a learnable per-channel scale. A lightweight
decoder reconstructs the mask using stride-4 and stride-2 encoder skips.
Training combines weighted cross-entropy and soft clDice on the main output
with a training-only stride-16 semantic deep-supervision loss. The current
architecture contains no detail-to-semantic residual and no centerline-Tversky
auxiliary head.

Under the native-resolution evaluation protocol described above, the final
Massachusetts and DeepGlobe results should be inserted after the current V4
experiments are complete: [IOU\_MASS] and [IOU\_DG]. The training-form model
contains 22.392~M parameters and its current re-parameterized implementation
contains 22.353~M stored parameters. Final GMAC and latency measurements should
be profiled from the same trained V4 checkpoint used for reporting accuracy.

Future work may extend the raster output to an explicit road graph and examine
whether the same asymmetric semantic-guidance strategy generalizes to
multispectral, SAR, or multi-temporal imagery.
